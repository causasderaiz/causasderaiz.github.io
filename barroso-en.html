No. 47 ⚖ In court Lithium mining

Lithium mine in Covas do Barroso

Boticas, Vila Real, Trás-os-Montes, Portugal (Barroso region)

⚖ Court cases pending · Government invoked public interest and works resumed · Commons reject Savannah's agreements

The people of Covas do Barroso, in Boticas (Trás-os-Montes, Portugal), are fighting the open-pit lithium mine that Savannah Resources wants to open in a centuries-old farming area. In June 2026, the Mirandela Administrative and Tax Court suspended the easement granting access to the commons, but the Government invoked the public interest and works resumed; in August, the Commons of Covas do Barroso said Savannah's agreements with other communities do not amount to social consent.

British company Savannah Resources wants to open an open-pit lithium mine in a centuries-old farming area, classified as a Globally Important Agricultural Heritage System by the FAO. Covas do Barroso has resisted the project for eight years.

On 29 May 2026, the Administrative and Tax Court of Mirandela admitted a precautionary injunction filed by the Assembly of Commoners of Covas do Barroso parish against the Ministry of Environment and Energy, suspending the effects of a 228-hectare administrative easement in Covas do Barroso and Romaínho and the geotechnical works that depended on it. Savannah Resources confirmed on 9 June that it had been notified of the temporary suspension order, saying it would resume work once authorised; the company noted this was the third precautionary injunction filed against the project since 2024.

On 29 June 2026, after 20 days of suspension, the Secretary of State for Energy, Jean Paulo Gil Barroca, signed a reasoned resolution invoking the project's public interest and allowing the geotechnical works to resume. On 30 June, the Directive Council of the Commons of Covas do Barroso said the resolution ignored violations reported by the community, including tree felling outside the periods authorised for the protection of birds and the Iberian wolf, in breach of the Environmental Impact Statement, and warned that if invoking "strategic interest" is enough to continue acts whose legality the courts are still assessing, it sets "a deeply concerning precedent".

On 14 August 2026, after Savannah announced agreements with the commons of Alijó, Canedo and Penalonga (Ribeira de Pena, 150 hectares), an amendment to its 2020 contract with the Commons of Couto de Dornelas (Boticas) and a memorandum with the Secretariat of the Commons of Trás-os-Montes and Alto Douro, the Directive Council of the Commons of Covas do Barroso stated that "financial compensation, protocols or community benefits do not turn these agreements into social consent". It recalled that Savannah's proposal had been rejected at the Assembly of Commoners "by an overwhelming majority of those present": "Savannah obtained agreements with certain entities. It did not obtain the consent of Barroso".

In September 2026, Savannah reported that it had dug 189 geotechnical test pits on site and, on 23 September, it completed a capital increase of 31.3 million euros. According to the company, the Environmental Compliance Report for the detailed design (RECAPE) is due in the coming months and construction is expected to start in the first half of 2027.

From August 6 to 10, 2026, between 700 and 800 people take part in the Camp in Defence of Barroso, organized by Unidos em Defesa de Covas do Barroso with support from A Sachola, Disgraça and Jornal Mapa.

In February 2026, Unidos em Defesa de Covas do Barroso and ClientEarth brought an action against the European Commission before the Court of Justice of the European Union, challenging the decision to grant the Barroso mine "strategic project" status under the Critical Raw Materials Act. According to the two organisations, the Commission ignored documented risks to water, ecosystems, human health and local livelihoods.

The conditionally favourable Environmental Impact Statement issued by the Portuguese Environment Agency on 31 May 2023 required the project to stop any water abstraction from the Covas river, with a protection zone of at least 100 metres on each side of the riverbed, a compensation plan for oak woodland (habitat 9230), a compensation plan for the red-billed chough and the creation of a breeding centre for freshwater pearl mussel (Margaritifera margaritifera) on the Beça river. The planned concession covers 593 hectares, with an estimated lifetime of 17 years.
