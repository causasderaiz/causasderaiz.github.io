Moratorium on deep-sea mining in Azorean waters

Waters under the management of the Autonomous Region of the Azores

✓ Legal moratorium until 2050 · Law 36/2025 enacted

Environmental organisations warned that deep-sea mining is "highly threatening" to the marine biodiversity of the Azores, home to hydrothermal fields and deep-sea endemic species. After the Azores Legislative Assembly recommended a moratorium in 2023, Portugal's national Parliament passed Law 36/2025 in March 2025, banning deep-sea mining in all waters under Portuguese jurisdiction, including the Azores, until 1 January 2050. Portugal became the first country in the world with a legally binding moratorium on this activity.

The deep waters around the Azores include hydrothermal fields and seamounts with endemic species and still poorly studied ecosystems, which environmental organisations identify as particularly vulnerable to deep-sea mining: the extraction of polymetallic nodules, crusts and sulphides from the ocean floor. In 2024, organisations including Sciaena, ANP|WWF and the Azores Ocean Observatory (OMA) warned that this activity is "highly threatening" to the region's marine biodiversity, and criticised the Azores Maritime Spatial Plan for being silent on an effective ban.

In May 2023, the Legislative Assembly of the Autonomous Region of the Azores approved a resolution recommending that the regional government apply a moratorium on deep-sea mining in maritime zones under the Region's management, until 1 January 2050. Pressure from these organisations, including a petition delivered to the Azores Parliament by Natureza Portugal/WWF and Sciaena, helped bring the issue to the national Parliament.

On 14 March 2025, the Assembly of the Republic approved a national moratorium on deep-sea mining until 2050. The law was enacted by the President of the Republic on 21 March and published as Law 36/2025 in the official gazette on 31 March, entering into force on 1 April 2025. It bans the activity in all maritime zones under national jurisdiction, including Azorean waters, until 1 January 2050, making Portugal the first country in the world to adopt a legally binding moratorium on this activity.
