Kaolin and clay mine in Serra d'El-Rei, Peniche

Serra d'El-Rei, Peniche municipality, Leiria district

⚡ OesteCIM (12 municipalities) asks APA for an unfavourable environmental impact declaration (Sep 2026)

Open-pit kaolin and clay mining planned for a 101-hectare area in the Serra d'El-Rei parish, state-conceded for two decades to Motamineral. The Oeste Intermunicipal Community, grouping 12 municipalities, asked the Portuguese Environment Agency for an unfavourable Environmental Impact Declaration, flagging errors in the project's location data (the assessment places homes 4 km away, when houses actually stand 20, 150 and 450 metres from the site) and risks to 24 groundwater catchments and the São Domingos reservoir.

The "Royal China Clay" project, run by Motamineral, Minerais Industriais, S.A., involves open-pit kaolin and clay mining across 101 hectares in the Serra d'El-Rei parish, Peniche municipality, under a two-decade state concession. Within the area, three cores total 51.4 hectares of active mining, with projected annual output of around 569,100 tonnes (31,800 tonnes of kaolin, 270,000 of washed sand and 267,300 of common clay).

On 28 August 2026, Quercus asked the Portuguese Environment Agency to reject the project, warning of dust, aquifer contamination and threats to cork oaks. On 1 September, the Portuguese Association of Landscape Architects issued an unfavourable opinion, noting that 471 of the 662 inventoried cork oaks would be felled, with the recovery plan not including the planting of a single replacement cork oak.

On 11 September 2026, the council of the Oeste Intermunicipal Community (OesteCIM), grouping 12 municipalities in the region, decided to formally oppose the project and asked APA for an unfavourable Environmental Impact Declaration, calling it "incompatible with the environmental and territorial support capacity of the surrounding area". OesteCIM points to errors in the Environmental Impact Assessment regarding distances to nearby homes (the assessment states 4 km, when houses actually stand 20, 150 and 450 metres from the site), directly affecting the evaluation of noise, dust and public health impacts, and warns of risk to 24 groundwater catchments and the São Domingos reservoir, near which a sand-washing plant is planned. The council also estimates the mine would create only 12 direct jobs, against the environmental and traffic costs (11 trucks per hour projected) for the region.

The municipalities of Peniche, Óbidos and Bombarral also gave unfavourable opinions. Peniche's mayor announced he would request meetings with the Environment Minister and APA.
