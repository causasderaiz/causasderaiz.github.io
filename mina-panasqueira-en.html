Panasqueira Mines: risk of toxic contamination threatens the Zêzere River

Covilhã and Fundão, Beira Baixa

⚡ Quercus opposes environmental licence renewal · Water intake suspended for 6 months

A collapse at a century-old mine's tailings dam sent lead and arsenic into the Ribeira de Cebola, a tributary that feeds the Castelo de Bode reservoir, the main source of water treated by EPAL for around 3 million people in Greater Lisbon. On the day the public consultation on the mine's Environmental Licence (PCIP) renewal closed, Quercus backed MiningWatch Portugal's unfavourable technical opinion and is asking the Portuguese Environment Agency not to renew the licence without a scheduled, financially guaranteed closure plan; six months after the incident, the water intake remains suspended and monitoring results have not been published.

The Panasqueira Mines, operating for over a century across the municipalities of Covilhã and Fundão, are among Europe's oldest and most productive tin and tungsten mines, currently run by Beralt Tin and Wolfram Portugal. In January 2026, heavy rain caused another collapse in the mine's tailings structure, sweeping toxic inert waste into the Cebola stream, a tributary of the Zêzere River.

Quercus warned of highly toxic heavy metals, including lead and arsenic, also detected along the banks of the Zêzere itself. The incident led to the suspension of water intake at a local supply point and forced Covilhã's municipal council to step up water quality testing.

The association notes that the Castelo do Bode Dam, on the Zêzere, supplies water to the Greater Lisbon area, and is calling on the Portuguese Environment Agency, the water regulator ERSAR and the Inspectorate-General of Agriculture, Sea, Environment and Land Use Planning for stronger oversight, while demanding that Beralt remediate the tailings, expand wastewater treatment capacity and carry out regular maintenance of the pipes carrying toxic effluent.
