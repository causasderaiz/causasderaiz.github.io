Peneda-Gerês National Park: petition calls for more protection

Citizens' petition asks Parliament for stronger protection of Peneda-Gerês National Park

Peneda-Gerês National Park (Mata da Albergaria, Terras de Bouro)

⚡ Citizens' petition to Parliament

⚡ Sign the petition

A group of citizens who use Peneda-Gerês National Park is asking Parliament to stop the Park's degradation: roads closed to organised events, a director chosen by open competition, more resources for ICNF and nature wardens, and visitor limits on roads, viewpoints and waterfalls. The petition came after the Tour of Portugal crossed the Mata da Albergaria, the best-preserved native oak forest in the country.

What is at stake

Peneda-Gerês is Portugal's only national park and part of the Gerês-Xurés Transboundary Biosphere Reserve recognised by UNESCO. In the Homem river valley lies the Mata da Albergaria, the best-preserved native oak forest in the country, with centuries-old oaks, yews, Iberian wolves, Pyrenean desmans and gold-striped salamanders. The petition points out that a 2023 law requires a director for the Park to be appointed by open competition, which has not yet happened.

The Albergaria woods. More than 1,500 hectares of oak woodland in the Homem valley, the best-preserved native forest in the country and a Council of Europe Biogenetic Reserve. It holds about 90% of Portugal's yew population, as well as the Portuguese laurel, a relic of the Tertiary period.

The animals of the Homem valley. The woods are home to the Iberian wolf, Pyrenean desman, otter, honey buzzard, the gold-striped salamander and Iberian emerald lizard, both Iberian endemics, and the stag beetle, which depends on the dead wood of mature oaks.

Visitor pressure. According to Quercus, the woods already bear heavy car and tourist pressure, and there is no public assessment of their carrying capacity or of the cumulative effects of visits. The Volta a Portugal cycle race crossed them on 13 August 2026.
