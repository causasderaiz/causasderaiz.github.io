October 2017 wildfires: families still waiting for their homes to be rebuilt

Centre Region (Coimbra, Viseu and Guarda districts), with the movement based in Midões, Tábua

⚡ About 20 families still waiting for their homes; movement considers a complaint against the State at the European Court of Human Rights

Almost ten years after the wildfires of 15 October 2017, about 20 families in the Centre Region still have not had their main homes rebuilt, according to MAAVIM. In some cases the works were contracted and paid for, but the houses were never built. The movement, which tried unsuccessfully to join the criminal case over the fires, is considering taking the Portuguese State to the European Court of Human Rights.

The wildfires of 15 October 2017 hit Portugal's Centre Region. The Midões Fire Victims Support Association (MAAVIM), in Tábua, defends people harmed by those fires.

In October 2023, according to Lusa, MAAVIM appealed to the Supreme Court of Justice after the Coimbra Court of Appeal refused to let it join, as an assisting party, the criminal case over the October 2017 fires in Coimbra district, which the Public Prosecutor had closed. The courts found that the movement lacks standing because it is not an environmental protection association. "We want to make sure there is a trial and that this does not end with the case being closed", the movement's spokesperson said at the time.

On 4 May 2026, according to MAAVIM figures reported by JN, about 20 families in the Centre Region, in the Coimbra, Viseu and Guarda districts, were still waiting for their main homes to be rebuilt. According to the movement, in some cases the works were contracted, with contracts signed, companies paid and supervision in place, but the houses were never built. Some delays are due to legalisation problems or to victims being unable to deal with the paperwork. Some families are living with relatives and others have left the country. The spokesperson, Nuno Tavares Pereira, told Renascença the families "are willing to do anything", after so much time "without answers": "There is no reason for these cases not to have been done, when others were done where nobody lived and the homes were rebuilt". MAAVIM is considering a complaint against the Portuguese State at the European Court of Human Rights once national remedies are exhausted, and had a meeting scheduled for 19 May with the Parliamentary Inquiry Committee on the Rural Fires Business.

According to ZAP, citing JN, the Centre Regional Coordination and Development Commission received 1,340 applications for support, of which 849 qualified under the Permanent Housing Recovery Support Programme, has cases pending in Pampilhosa da Serra, Poiares and Vouzela, and says it is working to complete them by 2026.
