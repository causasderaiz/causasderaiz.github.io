Mouth of the Jamor: Porto Cruz towers on a floodplain in Cruz Quebrada

Right bank of the mouth of the Jamor river, Cruz Quebrada, Oeiras municipality, Lisbon district

⚖ With the Detailed Plan in court since 2021 (Public Prosecutor action), the Porto Cruz subdivision awaits a council decision

The Detailed Plan for the Right Bank of the Mouth of the Jamor River, approved in 2014, paves the way for Porto Cruz: 17- and 19-storey towers, 355 homes, a hotel, offices and more than 1,600 parking spaces on the site of former factories in Cruz Quebrada, by the Tagus. The Vamos Salvar o Jamor (Let's Save the Jamor) association opposes the project because it sits on a floodplain and in an area hit by coastal overtopping; in 2021, the Public Prosecutor asked a court to declare provisions of the plan illegal.

The Detailed Plan for the Right Bank of the Mouth of the Jamor River was published in 2014, by decision of Oeiras Municipal Assembly, and frames the licensing application for the Porto Cruz subdivision, on the site of former factories at the mouth of the Jamor, in Cruz Quebrada, by the Tagus and the Jamor National Sports Centre.

In February 2021, with the subdivision under public consultation, Oeiras council said the marina was essential to the development: according to New in Oeiras, the project includes five plots with six residential buildings, a hotel, offices and shops, the tallest with 19 storeys, and 1,639 parking spaces. The association warned of flood risk, more traffic and the impact on the landscape of the Jamor valley.

On 30 March 2021, the Public Prosecutor announced it had brought an administrative action and, at the same time, an injunction to have provisions of the Detailed Plan declared illegal; the court provisionally suspended those provisions and the activities carried out under them, and the public consultation on the subdivision was halted. The decision on the injunction became final on 24 July 2024, without keeping the suspension in place; according to the Vamos Salvar o Jamor association, the Public Prosecutor's main action against the Detailed Plan is still awaiting judgment. Following this, on 24 February 2025, the council resumed the public consultation on the subdivision, requested by Aziprime Property Developer, SA.

On 20 February 2026, according to Lusa, the association called on the council to back away from developing the right bank of the Jamor, which would bring "towers of 17 and 19 storeys, 355 homes and more than 1,600 parking spaces" to an area the Municipal Master Plan identifies as "floodplain, estuary protection strip and erosion risk". It recalled that the Public Prosecutor "asked for the whole detailed plan to be declared illegal", accused the mayor of holding back the results of the public consultation "for 11 months", and argued that recent coastal overtopping and floods show that dense urban development is incompatible with safety in the area.

In August 2026, according to Lusa, trees and vegetation were cut on the former Gist-Brocades site, between the EN6 road and Cruz Quebrada station. The association criticised the indiscriminate felling of more than 30 native trees, including pines and plane trees, and warned that moving all that vegetation "can carry sediments with it, which is harmful to public health". The Working Conditions Authority said it found no breaches of labour law and would not say whether asbestos removal had been requested. Oeiras council confirmed that the subdivision "has not been submitted to the municipal executive", and that the public consultation report had been sent to the developers.
