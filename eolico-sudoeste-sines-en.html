No. 67 ⚡ Active situation Large-scale wind farm

The Sudoeste wind farm plans up to 31 turbines and a battery park in Santiago do Cacém and Cercal do Alentejo to power the Sines data centre

Santiago do Cacém and Cercal do Alentejo, Alentejo coast

⚡ Public consultation on the assessment scope closed on 18 August

Up to 31 turbines and a lithium battery park across 1,098 hectares, to power the Sines Data Center that could eventually use 20% of the country's electricity. The public consultation on the scope of the environmental impact study closed on August 18; the full environmental impact study now follows.

What is at stake

Hyperion Renewables Sines wants to build the Southwest Wind Farm between Santiago do Cacém and Cercal do Alentejo: 23 to 31 turbines, 138 MW and a battery system, over a study area of 1,098.4 hectares, to supply the Sines data centre.

Cork oak woodland and heath. According to Quercus, the area has cork and holm oak woodland, European dry heaths, strawberry tree scrub and willow stands along the streams, all classified natural habitats, plus REN and RAN land. The Southwest Alentejo and Vicentine Coast Natural Park is 500 metres away and the Costa Sudoeste SAC 1.9 km away.

Rare plants. The developer's scoping proposal admits 50 rare, endemic or threatened plant species, 18 of them found only in Portugal. Five are European conservation priorities: Armeria rouyana, violet cress (Jonopsidium acaule), Linaria bipunctata subsp. glutinosa, Ononis hackelii and camphor thyme (Thymus camphoratus). On the Red List of vascular flora, Herniaria algarvica and autumn mandrake (Mandragora autumnalis) are Endangered, and Portuguese sundew (Drosophyllum lusitanicum), Ephedra fragilis subsp. fragilis, Juncus emmanuelis, Lavatera mauritanica subsp. davaei, Limonium dodartii, Myosotis retusifolia and Ononis hackelii are Vulnerable.

The birds. Up to 123 bird species may use the area, ten of them threatened in mainland Portugal according to the 2022 Red Book: cattle egret, purple heron, stone-curlew, long-eared owl, Bonelli's eagle, kestrel, hobby, Iberian grey shrike, woodchat shrike and black-eared wheatear; five were confirmed on site. SPEA points out that soaring birds, especially raptors, are among those most often killed by collisions with wind turbines and power lines.

Bats and mammals. Twelve bat species may occur. ICNF requires searches for dead bats around every turbine and pays special attention to species that fly above the tree canopy, such as Pipistrellus, Savi's pipistrelle and the noctules (Nyctalus). Five threatened mammals may also be present: Granada hare, European rabbit (confirmed), southwestern water vole, Cabrera's vole and polecat.

The streams. Quercus points to direct interference with watercourses: silting, higher turbidity and contamination from oil and fuel spills.

The people of Santiago do Cacém and Sines. Quercus points to turbine noise as a threat to residents' quality of life.
