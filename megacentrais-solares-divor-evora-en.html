Divor mega solar plants, north of Évora

Graça do Divor and the area north and northeast of Évora, partly in Arraiolos, Évora district

⚡ Three plants planned next to the Divor substation · platform took the case to the Portuguese UNESCO Commission and ICOMOS · petition open

The civic platform Juntos pelo Divor is contesting three mega photovoltaic plants planned for the area north and northeast of Évora, next to the Divor substation: the Incognitworld 3 plant, the Graça do Divor plant (Hyperion Renewables) and Sol de Évora (Newcon40). According to the platform, together they add up to more than 1,550,000 panels on more than 1,700 contiguous hectares and put at risk the World Heritage status of Évora's historic centre.

North and northeast of Évora, in the Graça do Divor area and partly in the municipality of Arraiolos, three mega photovoltaic plants are planned, all connected to the Divor substation: the Incognitworld 3 plant, with 362,076 modules; the Graça do Divor Photovoltaic Plant, by Hyperion Renewables, with 394,500 panels; and the Sol de Évora plant, by Newcon40 Unipessoal Lda, with 800,100 modules. According to Lusa, only the Incognitworld 3 plant is licensed.

The Sol de Évora plant was presented in March 2025, when the scoping proposal for its environmental impact study was in public consultation on the Participa portal until 24 March. According to the developer, it would produce about 848.5 GWh a year and have a storage system in 48 containers. The application was made to DGEG in 2019.

The civic platform Juntos pelo Divor: Paisagem e Património (Together for Divor: Landscape and Heritage) launched a petition on 27 June 2024, addressed to the Minister of Environment and Energy, the president of CCDR Alentejo and the mayor of Évora, against the installation of mega solar plants in the parish of Graça do Divor. The platform says it does not oppose the energy transition, but argues that the plants should be installed in less visible places close to existing infrastructure.

August 2025: after a meeting with the company on 8 July 2025, the platform sent a letter calling the Graça do Divor and Sol de Évora projects "indefensible", with impacts that, it says, cannot be "avoided, mitigated or compensated", and writing that "the meeting with you was disappointing, it was conducted as if it were already a done deal".

1 April 2026: the platform warned that the UNESCO World Heritage status of Évora's historic centre may be at risk, because the three plants, with more than 1,550,000 panels on more than 1,700 contiguous hectares, contradict the integrity of the rural landscape north of the city, one of the requirements of the 1986 classification. It took the case to the Portuguese National Commission for UNESCO and to ICOMOS Portugal, and is asking for a heritage impact assessment and for Évora to be included on the list of heritage in danger. In May 2026 the platform released a poster with the slogan "This is not energy transition, this is the destruction of our heritage".

12 June 2026: the Évora Municipal Assembly held a public hearing on the three plants, to which it invited APA, ICNF, the Portuguese National Commission for UNESCO and the companies Hyperion Renewables and Newcon40. The projects will still have to go through public consultation before a final decision.

15 July 2026: Grupo Pro-Évora and the Juntos pelo Divor platform contested the proposed Sectoral Programme for Renewable Energy Acceleration Zones, which would designate as an acceleration zone the entire northern part of the municipality within 10 kilometres of the Divor substation. Both are asking for a heritage impact assessment before any approval.
