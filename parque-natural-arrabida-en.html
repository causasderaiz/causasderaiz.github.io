Quarries, building and tourism put pressure on Arrábida Natural Park, where 11 quarries already cover 323 hectares, in Sesimbra, Setúbal and Palmela

Serra da Arrábida: Sesimbra, Setúbal and Palmela

⚡ Simultaneous pressure on several fronts, with no updated plan since the park was created

Created in 1976, Arrábida Natural Park protects 12,328 hectares and forms part of the Arrábida/Espichel Special Conservation Area (Natura 2000), with 32 protected habitat types. Yet no instrument today assesses the cumulative pressure surrounding it: 11 quarries already occupy 323 hectares inside the park (more than double the area in 1976, only two of which are being rehabilitated), including Secil's, which runs one of the country's largest cement plants there; a court case disputes public ownership of five beaches in Setúbal ("Hands Off Arrábida!"); the Forte Velho do Outão fort was granted as a 50-year concession to a hotel group; and four tourism megaprojects in Meco and Azóia, with over 4,000 planned beds, are moving forward without a cumulative Environmental Impact Assessment, despite sharing the same protected area, the reason behind the Meco Livre Movement's "SOS Meco" petition. The Frade cave spider, Europe's smallest and critically endangered, survives in a single cave in the range threatened by these same quarries. According to the association Arrábida - Natureza e Comunidade, the built-up area inside the park has grown by around 500% since the 1960s.

What is at stake

The Arrábida Natural Park protects 12,328 hectares of limestone hills by the sea, between Setúbal, Palmela and Sesimbra. Quarries now cover 323 hectares, double the 1976 area, and built-up land inside the park has grown by about 500% since the 1960s. Quarrying, building and tourism press on the same territory; some of these conflicts have their own case file, such as Meco Livre and the Outão Fort.

The caves. The Frade cave spider, Critically Endangered according to IUCN, lives only in the Frade karst system, which is threatened by quarries. The Ribeiro do Cavalo no. 3 quarry is 280 metres from the Zambujal Cave, a shelter of national importance where Schreibers' bats hibernate, and the nature conservation authority found the study claiming blasting does not affect them lacking in scientific rigour.

The hills. On the expansion of Secil's Vale de Mós quarry, LPN concluded there are very significant direct conflicts with flora and priority habitats for nature conservation. Quarries already cover 323 hectares of the park, twice the 1976 area.

The Meco dunes. In the same SAC, at Meco, the study for one of the tourism developments confirmed priority dune habitats and endemics such as Portuguese thyme, sea rupturewort and Ulex densus, and in 2021 CCDR proposed an unfavourable decision because it would irreversibly damage priority habitats and endemic vegetation (Meco Livre case).
