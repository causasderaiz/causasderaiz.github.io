Mine planned 69 metres from homes in Juncal, Porto de Mós

José Aldeia Lagoa & Filhos plans an open-pit mine 69 metres from homes in Juncal, Porto de Mós

Juncal, Porto de Mós (Leiria district), between the village, Cumeira de Cima and the EN8 road

🗳 Public consultation until 14 December 2026 · EIA procedure no. 3935 (APA)

🗳 Take part in the public consultation

José Aldeia Lagoa & Filhos, a company from Pombal, has submitted the environmental impact assessment for the Juncal 2 mining concession: 129.5 hectares in the parish of Juncal, with 41 hectares dug open-pit for almost 18 years, or 61 hectares for 29 years if the municipal master plan changes, to remove 240,000 m³ a year of sand, kaolin, red clay, crushed stone and gravel. The nearest pit is 69 metres from the hamlet of Cumeira de Cima and 182 metres from the village. The public consultation run by APA, the Portuguese Environment Agency, is open on participa.pt until 14 December 2026.

What is at stake

Scenario 2, with 2.6 million m³ more, only goes ahead if the Porto de Mós master plan is changed and, according to the additional information, runs past the 30-year term of the contract, which assumes it will be extended. The public consultation takes submissions on participa.pt until 14 December 2026.

People in Cumeira de Cima and Juncal. The pits lie 69 metres from Cumeira de Cima, less than 200 metres from the village and 301 metres from the school, and shipping puts about 40 loaded lorries a day on the road. The study itself records that near the Cumeira de Cima school noise already exceeds the limits for sensitive areas, with a contribution from the mine, and proposes earth barriers near the houses and a free yearly respiratory screening for nearby residents.

The water beneath the sand. The soils are sandy and let rainwater soak in, and the plants use about 47,000 m³ of fresh water a year, taken from wells and a flooded old quarry. The assessment committee replied that the groundwater parameters measured in the study "are not representative of the impacts potentially caused by extractive activity". The ponds left by old pits are to be fenced with drowning-danger signs.

Cork oaks and ponds. Mining requires felling 138 cork oaks, a tree protected by law, over about 1.5 hectares, offset by planting in other municipalities (Ansião, Pombal, Leiria and Porto de Mós). According to the study, the ponds and small wetlands are the most valuable habitats, where the pygmy marbled newt (Triturus pygmaeus) breeds, a species near threatened worldwide, according to the study. The Serras de Aire e Candeeiros Natura 2000 site lies just over 1 km away.
