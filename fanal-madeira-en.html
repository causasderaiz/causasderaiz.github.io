Fanal: grazing and overtourism threaten centuries-old forest

Fanal, Madeira Natural Park, Porto Moniz

◐ Quercus reports grazing and trampling · Government recommends alternative routes

Fanal is a Rest and Silence Zone within Madeira Natural Park, known for its centuries-old til trees. Quercus reports that cattle grazing freely devour the trees' new shoots, and that excessive visitor numbers compact the soil and expose roots, with no carrying capacity or marked trails defined by the Institute of Forests and Nature Conservation (IFCN). The Regional Government acknowledged the problem and recommended alternative routes to tour operators, but the association considers the measure insufficient.

Fanal, within Madeira Natural Park (created in 1982), is classified as a Rest and Silence Zone, a protection category that, by definition, should limit motorised traffic and reserve the space for "quiet recreation". It is known for its centuries-old til trees, emblematic of the Laurissilva forest, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

In September 2025, the Madeira Regional Branch of Quercus warned of a "slow death" of this protected zone: cattle grazing freely devour the shoots of the centuries-old til trees, and excessive visitor numbers lead to parking that exceeds the capacity of the two existing car parks, with rental and off-road vehicles crowding the road verges. Dozens of visitors trample the reserve's soil with no marked trails, compacting it to the point that herbaceous plants no longer grow in some areas, exposing tree roots.

A year earlier, in September 2024, the same association had already criticised the Institute of Forests and Nature Conservation (IFCN) for authorising a wedding with over 200 guests inside this Rest and Silence Zone, complete with a marquee and litter left on site, a case the GNR police went on to investigate.

Quercus proposes defining the area's carrying capacity, creating marked and signposted footpaths, strengthening surveillance, evaluating parking alternatives and limiting grazing in sensitive areas. The Regional Government acknowledged the problem of excessive visitation and recommended alternative routes to tour operators, a measure the association considers insufficient given the severity of the situation.
