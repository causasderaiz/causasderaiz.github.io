Bairro da Penajóia: Cape Verdean, Angolan and Guinean families evicted without rehousing in Almada

Penajóia, Monte da Caparica, Almada

⚡ Demolitions continue · IHRU promises no eviction without a housing solution

About 350 people from Portuguese-speaking African countries, 60 of them children, live on state-owned land without public electricity, water or sanitation in Almada, while demolitions without rehousing continue.

The Penajóia neighbourhood, in Monte da Caparica, grew mainly over the past two years, on land belonging to IHRU, the Institute for Housing and Urban Rehabilitation. The housing crisis and unaffordable rents pushed families from Cape Verde, Angola and Guinea-Bissau, along with Brazilian and Portuguese families, into self-construction. About 160 households, roughly 350 people, now live in the neighbourhood, including 60 children, six newborns, 17 teenagers, elderly people, chronically ill residents and pregnant women, without public electricity, piped water or sanitation.

IHRU began demolishing unfinished structures in January 2025. In December of that year, organizations again reported a case of forced demolition and eviction without rehousing: a family with three minor children was left without adequate housing after a demolition, and was even threatened with having the children removed while staying temporarily in a guesthouse.

In December 2025, Almada City Council announced that the Ministry of Infrastructure and Housing would schedule a meeting for January to present a plan to halt the neighbourhood's expansion and find a housing solution. IHRU insists there will be no demolitions without alternative housing solutions, but residents, who wrote a letter to the council titled "We are Almada too", remain under threat.

The same pattern documented in Beja and Loures repeats here: a racialized community, mostly of African origin, without access to basic environmental infrastructure, water, electricity, sanitation, and subject to evictions without a guaranteed housing alternative. This is environmental racism, even though the Portuguese press mostly frames it as a housing crisis.
