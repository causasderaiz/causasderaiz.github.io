Fundão Biomass Plant: seven years of noise, smoke and ash at Sítio da Gramenesa

Sítio da Gramenesa, Fundão, Beira Baixa

⚡ Residents report breach of the 2025 agreement · Plant rejects accusations (Apr 2026)

Since 2019, the residents of Gramenesa have lived next door to a biomass plant that has brought them noise, smoke, dust and ash. A 2025 court agreement promised solutions, but residents say little has changed; the company rejects the accusations of non-compliance.

The Fundão Biomass Plant (CBF) started operating in 2019 in the Fundão Industrial Zone, next to Sítio da Gramenesa. Since then, residents have reported noise, smoke, dust and ash. In March 2021, Quercus warned of the new plant's "evident negative impacts", pointing to high noise levels and dust released by wood chipping, and called for urgent action from the Environment Minister.

In January 2025, the Sítio da Gramenesa Residents' Association and the company signed an agreement that closed the ongoing court cases. The plant committed to complying with noise legislation, with night-time restrictions, carrying out annual measurements, monitoring biomass chipping, ash handling and air emissions, installing noise barriers, carrying out works in homes and paying compensation.

In March 2026, a SÁBADO report showed that, seven years after start-up, residents still live with noise, dust and open-air ash and report that the agreement has been breached. On 8 April 2026, the company rejected the accusations and said it had invested more than one million euros in measures such as acoustic panels, window insulation, dust control and compensation for residents. It blamed the delays on some residents refusing access to their properties, which prevented noise measurements and planned works, and on the Municipal Council which, according to the company, said it will not go ahead with a solution set out in the agreement. On 10 April, the CDU demanded that residents' rights be respected and that the competent authorities take responsibility for inspections.

A study published in 2026 in the journal Análise Social, by Luís Silva, uses this case as an example of environmental injustice in the energy transition: the plant's costs and benefits are unequally distributed, to the detriment of residents; they had no access to prior information about the project nor took part in the decision; and they were not recognised as legitimate stakeholders.
