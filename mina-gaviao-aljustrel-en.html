Gavião mine in Aljustrel: ZERO calls for the Almina project to be rejected

Aljustrel and Rio de Moinhos, Beja district

◐ Public consultation closed on 31 August · ZERO calls for the project to be rejected

The C-14 “Gavião” mining concession, held by Almina – Minas do Alentejo, would exploit a new deposit of sulphur, iron, copper, lead, zinc, silver and gold next to the existing Aljustrel mine, with operations planned until 2050. The public consultation on the environmental impact study closed on 31 August; the ZERO association argues the project does not meet the conditions for approval, since it fails to assess a rail alternative for transporting the ore (even though Aljustrel's ore has been carried by train for decades), and does not present a destination for the millions of tonnes of waste to be produced after 2031.

The Gavião mine project represents a total investment of around 250 million euros by Almina – Minas do Alentejo, involving the construction of a new underground mine next to the existing Aljustrel mine, reusing the current processing plant and other facilities. The company plans to extract around 1.5 million tonnes of ore per year, mostly copper, but also zinc, plus smaller quantities of sulphur, iron, lead, silver and gold. If it goes ahead, the investment is expected to create around 150 direct and indirect jobs and extend operations at the Aljustrel mines until 2050.

Once local processing is complete, the project plans to export around 85,000 tonnes of copper and zinc concentrates a year by truck, through the ports of Setúbal and Huelva, in Spain, despite a rail branch line that historically served Aljustrel's ore transport, currently deactivated.

The environmental association ZERO issued an unfavourable opinion on the project during the public consultation, which closed on 31 August 2026, calling for the process to be rejected. Among the reasons cited: the environmental impact study does not properly assess a rail transport alternative for the ore, even though that was the historical solution for Aljustrel for decades; and the project does not present a defined destination for the millions of tonnes of waste (tailings and processing rejects) that will be produced from 2031 onward, once the capacity of existing waste piles runs out.
