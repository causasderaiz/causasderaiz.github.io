Hotel Rural Montes da Comporta: 200 beds in the pine forest and dunes of the Comporta/Galé site

Montes da Comporta, Alcácer do Sal (Comporta parish)

🗳 Public consultation until 12 November 2026 · AIA process 595 (CCDR Alentejo)

✉️ Take part by email (participa.pt is down)

Monte da Barrada do Moinho wants to build a rural hotel with 72 units and 200 beds on 17.6 hectares of pine forest, entirely inside the Comporta/Galé Natura 2000 site. The ICNF rejected the first version in 2018. The environmental impact study is in public consultation until 12 November.

What is at stake

The ICNF gave a negative opinion on the first project in September 2018, because it lay inside the Comporta/Galé site. The developer grouped the buildings and reduced the area taken, and the ICNF gave a positive opinion in March 2019. The Dunas Livres Association, which already opposed another rural hotel in the same area, at Herdade do Chão do Tojo, opposes the project. ZERO and other associations have warned for years about the over-use of aquifers as resorts and golf courses multiply in the area, and they oppose tourism projects in Sites of Community Importance: even small ones add up. By 6 November, the public consultation had received 138 submissions. Photo: Alentejo Ilustrado.

The pine forest and the dunes. The land is almost all pine forest, inside the Comporta/Galé Natura 2000 site. It holds a patch of a habitat that is a priority for Europe: fossil dunes with juniper scrub (Juniperus navicularis). The works clear about a quarter of the 17.6 hectares and fell the pine trees there for houses, paths and car parks.

The rare plants. The study counts 158 plant species, 22 of them rare, endemic, localised, threatened or endangered. Six of these were confirmed on site and may be affected by the works.

The shrikes, the kestrel and the wild rabbit. Five species with an unfavourable conservation status live on the land: the southern grey shrike, the woodchat shrike, the kestrel, the cattle egret and the wild rabbit. The hare and the peregrine falcon may also be there.

The water. With no public supply, the hotel would draw about 28,000 cubic metres of water a year from two boreholes, from an aquifer that ZERO describes as over-exploited by the resorts and golf courses of the Alentejo coast. The pools would be filled by tanker lorries.

People in Comporta. More traffic and noise by the access road, and more pressure on health services, especially in summer.
