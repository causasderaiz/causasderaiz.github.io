Terges e Cobres and Carreiras dams: new dams on the Guadiana's tributaries

Terges, Cobres and Carreiras streams, Beja and Mértola municipalities, Beja district

⚡ EDIA has opened a tender for the design and environmental impact study; ZERO demands it be suspended

In June 2026, EDIA, the company that runs Alqueva, opened a tender for the design and environmental impact study of the Terges e Cobres dam (Beja and Mértola) and announced a similar procedure for the Carreiras dam (Mértola), under the "Água que Une" (Water that Unites) strategy. ZERO is demanding the tenders be suspended and a commitment not to build new dams on the Guadiana's tributaries, streams that dry up in summer; Alqueva irrigators and Mértola council support the works.

In June 2026, the Alqueva Development and Infrastructure Company (EDIA) opened a public tender for the design and environmental impact study of the Terges e Cobres dam, in the municipalities of Beja and Mértola, worth 990,000 euros (plus VAT), and announced a tender for the Carreiras dam, in Mértola. Both projects, on the Terges, Cobres and Carreiras streams, tributaries of the Guadiana downstream of the Alqueva-Pedrógão system, are part of the national "Água que Une" (Water that Unites) strategy. EDIA's president, José Pedro Salema, argues that the dams will "ensure ecological flows in the lower Guadiana, relieving pressure on Alqueva".

On 20 June 2026, according to Lusa, ZERO demanded that the tenders be suspended, an independent post-assessment of the Alqueva Multi-Purpose Project before any new works, and "a formal commitment from the Government not to build new dams on the Guadiana's tributaries", saying that "desertification and water scarcity may be causing hallucinations among policymakers". According to ZERO, these streams have no flow in summer, in a region forecast to get less rain; the Guadiana basin already has a water exploitation index (WEI+) of 51%, among the highest in the country; about 95% of the water distributed by Alqueva serves private economic activities, and about 80% of the irrigated area is olive groves, in a country that already produces twice the olive oil it consumes. For the association, the dams serve to keep expanding agriculture.

On 24 June 2026, the Association of Alqueva Landowners and Beneficiaries (APBA) disputed ZERO's position, which it says shows a "disconnect between desk activism and the reality of a territory at risk", and argued that holding back floodwater "is not an attack on nature, but the only effective shield" against climate change. Mértola municipal council supports the investments.
