No. 63 ◐ Monitoring Tourism in protected area

Meco Livre

Meco and Azóia, Sesimbra, Setúbal district

◐ SOS Meco petition closed · Movement continues to denounce pine felling in protected area

Associative movement against the destruction of Sesimbra's protected areas for the construction of tourism developments.

The Meco Livre Movement is a civic group dedicated to defending and preserving the natural and landscape heritage of the Sesimbra municipality, with particular attention to the villages of Meco and Azóia. In June 2025 it launched the "SOS Meco: No to mega projects! Yes to Nature! Yes to a free Meco!" petition, warning of "one of the biggest environmental attacks ever seen in the Sesimbra region".

The target is four large-scale tourism developments (Etosoto, Idiluz, Pinhal da Prata and TURIMECO's "Pinhal do Atlântico" aparthotel), which together plan more than 3,000 tourist beds across an area equivalent to about 50 football pitches, threatening to destroy hundreds of hectares of pine forest in a village that in 2011 had fewer than 500 inhabitants. One of the developments sits inside Arrábida Natural Park; all of them fall within the Arrábida/Espichel Special Conservation Area (Natura 2000).

The movement argues that the four projects are moving forward without a cumulative Environmental Impact Assessment, a legally required instrument when multiple, contiguous interventions have synergistic impacts, under Decree-Law 151-B/2013. Sesimbra's town council maintains the projects comply with the Master Plan (in force since 1998) and each has a favourable Environmental Impact Assessment from the relevant authority (CCDR-LVT, ICNF, APA), but the movement insists that assessment was never done jointly for the four projects' combined impact.

In March 2026, the Meco Livre Movement also denounced the felling of stone pines at Herdade do Cabeço da Flauta, in Sesimbra. The nature conservation institute ICNF replied that the felling needed no authorisation, since it took place on private land outside the Special Conservation Area and outside the national network of protected areas, a response the movement considers inadequate given the area's ecological value.

The SOS Meco petition was delivered to the Portuguese Parliament on 24 February 2026, with 14,683 signatures, demanding the immediate suspension of licensing, a comprehensive and transparent cumulative Environmental Impact Assessment with effective public consultation, and stronger environmental and urban-planning oversight across the Sesimbra municipality.
