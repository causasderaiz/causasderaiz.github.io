No. 63 ⚡ Active situation Tourism in protected area

Four tourism projects with more than 3,000 beds planned still have no environmental impact assessment in the Meco protected area, Sesimbra

Meco and Azóia, Sesimbra, Setúbal district

⚡ SOS Meco petition delivered to Parliament with 14,683 signatures · the four projects still have no cumulative environmental impact assessment

Since 2021, the Meco Livre Movement and residents of Aldeia do Meco have opposed four large tourism projects (over 3,000 planned beds, including Turimeco's "Pinhal do Atlântico" aparthotel) in a protected area of Sesimbra. The SOS Meco petition, launched by the movement, was delivered to Parliament with 14,683 signatures, and in March 2026 the movement denounced the felling of stone pines at Cabeço da Flauta.

What is at stake

Four tourist developments (Etosoto, Idiluz, Pinhal da Prata and Turimeco's Pinhal do Atlântico aparthotel, with 411 units and 1,168 beds) plan more than 3,000 beds next to the villages of Meco and Azóia, in Sesimbra, all within the Arrábida/Espichel Natura 2000 Special Area of Conservation and one inside the Arrábida Natural Park.

The dunes and endemic plants. On the IDILUZ site, one of the four projects, the study confirmed priority habitats of grey dunes and fixed dunes with gorse, and plants found only in Portugal or Iberia: Portuguese thyme and sea rupturewort on the coastal sands (Annexes B-II and B-IV of the Habitats Directive), Thymus capitellatus and Thymus villosus (Annex B-IV), the gorses Ulex densus and Ulex australis subsp. welwitschianus, and 17 orchid species. It considered three more protected endemics very likely present: Armeria rouyana, Euphorbia transtagana and Iberis procumbens subsp. microcarpa. In 2021 CCDR proposed an unfavourable decision because the project would irreversibly damage priority habitats and endemic vegetation.

The pine woods. The four projects cover the equivalent of about 50 football pitches of pine woodland in the Arrábida/Espichel SAC, and in March 2026 the movement denounced the felling of stone pines at Cabeço da Flauta. Meco Livre warns of the irreversible destruction of priority habitats and contamination of soils and aquifers, with effects on the coast and local fishing.

The people of Meco. Over 3,000 beds in a village that, residents say, has neither the water network nor the roads for it, with more pressure on water and energy and the village turned into mass tourism.
