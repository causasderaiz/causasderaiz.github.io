Meco Livre

Meco and Azóia, Sesimbra, Setúbal district

⚡ New petition demands permit revocation over water salinization risk

Since 2021, the Meco Livre Movement and residents of Aldeia do Meco have opposed four large tourism projects (over 3,000 planned beds, including TURIMECO's "Pinhal do Atlântico" aparthotel) in a protected area of Sesimbra. After the SOS Meco petition (14,683 signatures, delivered to Parliament) and a denunciation of pine felling, a new petition now demands revocation of the licences over the risk of water salinization in the region.

The Meco Livre Movement is a civic group dedicated to defending and preserving the natural and landscape heritage of the Sesimbra municipality, with particular attention to the villages of Meco and Azóia. Since 2021, residents have opposed four large-scale tourism developments (Etosoto, Idiluz, Pinhal da Prata and TURIMECO's "Pinhal do Atlântico" aparthotel, with 411 units and 1,168 beds), which together plan more than 3,000 tourist beds and around 600 dwellings for a village with just over 300 inhabitants, across an area equivalent to about 50 football pitches. One of the developments sits inside Arrábida Natural Park; all of them fall within the Arrábida/Espichel Special Conservation Area (Natura 2000).

In June 2025, the Movement launched the "SOS Meco: No to mega projects! Yes to Nature! Yes to a free Meco!" petition, warning of "one of the biggest environmental attacks ever seen in the Sesimbra region", arguing that the four projects are moving forward without a cumulative Environmental Impact Assessment, a legally required instrument when multiple, contiguous interventions have synergistic impacts, under Decree-Law 151-B/2013. Sesimbra's town council maintains the projects comply with the Master Plan (in force since 1998) and each has a favourable Environmental Impact Assessment from the relevant authority (CCDR-LVT, ICNF, APA), but the Movement insists that assessment was never done jointly. The SOS Meco petition was delivered to the Portuguese Parliament on 24 February 2026, with 14,683 signatures, demanding the immediate suspension of licensing and stronger environmental and urban-planning oversight across the municipality.

In March 2026, the Movement also denounced the felling of stone pines at Herdade do Cabeço da Flauta, in Sesimbra. The nature conservation institute ICNF replied that the felling needed no authorisation, since it took place on private land outside the Special Conservation Area and outside the national network of protected areas, a response the Movement considers inadequate given the area's ecological value.

Most recent development: a new petition, filed by Aldeia do Meco residents, demands an embargo and revocation of the four developments' licences over the risk of salinizing the aquifer. According to official environment agency data cited in the petition, the water exploitation index in the coastal sub-basins between the Tagus and Sado reaches 93% in September, with the piezometric level of the Tagus/Left Bank basin already below the 20th percentile. This is the currently active petition.
