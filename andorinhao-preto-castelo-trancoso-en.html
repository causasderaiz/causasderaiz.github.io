No. 156 ⚡ Active situation Nests destroyed

Restoration work on Trancoso Castle's walls destroys common swift nests in the breeding season, and ICNF orders it stopped

Trancoso, Guarda district

⚡ ICNF ordered the works halted until the end of the 2026 nesting season · about 500 nests on the walls

The walls of Trancoso Castle hold the common swift colony that conservation groups say is probably the largest in Portugal. In April 2026, restoration works by the council and the company Monumenta went ahead in the middle of the breeding season, sealing the cracks and holes between the stones where the birds nest. The ICNF confirmed the direct destruction of nests and asked for the works to be halted during nesting.

What is at stake

The consolidation of the castle and walls is a Trancoso council project, carried out with the company Monumenta. Swifts arrive from Africa in mid-April and nest in the holes and cracks between the stones. According to the ICNF, the works should have applied for a licence under the exceptional regime of article 11 of Decree-Law 140/99. In Guimarães, wall cleaning stops during nesting; in Lourinhã, nests restrict the use of scaffolding.

The swifts. The common swift is a protected species in rapid decline in several European countries. It spends almost its whole life in the air and returns every year to the same crack in the wall to breed. Trancoso has about 500 nests on the walls and castle, and close to 2,000 swifts can be counted on a July day: the largest of the 350 colonies recorded in the last national survey by the Andorin project.

The wall. The restoration seals the holes and cracks between the stones, the only places where the colony nests. The groups say the destruction is irreversible and that the colony is part of Trancoso's cultural landscape. Part of the wall is already rendered with cement, where the birds cannot nest.

The breeding season. The works went ahead after the birds arrived, with eggs and chicks in the nests. The urgent request to the GNR environmental unit and the ICNF to stop them had no immediate effect.
