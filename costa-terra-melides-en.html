Costa Terra: luxury resort closes off public beaches in Melides

Melides, Grândola (coastline between Tróia and Melides)

⚡ Physical barriers unresolved · Quercus demands explanations (Aug 2026)

The luxury resort Costa Terra keeps private security and fencing restricting access to public beaches. An official inspection found seven beaches with conditioned access in Grândola; a year on, Quercus accuses the Government of hiding the fact that the barriers remain.

Between Tróia and Melides, in the municipality of Grândola, the luxury resort Costa Terra occupies an estate of around 850 hectares along the coast, developed by Discovery Land Company through CostaTerra, Sociedade Imobiliária de Grândola. Private security, fencing and ID checks restrict access to public beaches in the area, including Galé-Fontainhas beach, where for years visitors had to hand over their ID card at the entrance to a former campsite the company had acquired.

In July 2025, an inspection by the Portuguese Environment Agency, ordered by the Government, identified seven beaches in Grândola municipality with access conditioned by tourism developments: Camarinhas, Galé-Fontaínhas and Tróia-Galé already operating, and Duna Cinzenta, Golfinhos, Garças and Pinheirinho still under construction. The Government promised signage, new parking and public access corridors.

A year later, in August 2026, an investigative report again found unresolved physical barriers, particularly at Galé-Fontainhas and Aberta Nova beaches. Quercus publicly accused the Minister of Environment and the Environment Agency of "throwing sand in the eyes" of the Portuguese public, noting that a letter sent in June 2026 had never received a reply, and warned of the environmental cost of these developments: water scarcity, destruction of dune systems and disregard for coastal planning rules.

The Dunas Livres Association, founded in 2020 by biologists, a forestry engineer and an architect, has for years documented private security photographing visitors and licence plates near Costa Terra. After months without a response to requests for public documents about the development, the association took the case to the Administrative Court of Beja, which ruled in its favour against Grândola council. Along the 45 kilometres of coast between Tróia and Melides, a survey by Expresso found limited or non-existent access along about 80% of the shoreline, with beach umbrella rentals reaching €200 a day on the most sought-after beaches.
