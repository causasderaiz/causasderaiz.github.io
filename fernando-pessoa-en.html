Fernando Pessoa Solar Plant: Europe's largest keeps stalling in court

São Domingos, Santiago do Cacém, Alentejo coast

⚖ Licence suspended by court order

1,200 MW and 1.5 million trees to be felled for continental Europe's largest solar plant, its licence suspended by court order.

The Fernando Pessoa Photovoltaic Plant, developed by Iberdrola in partnership with Prosolia, was approved by the Portuguese Environment Agency in January 2023 as Europe's largest solar plant and the fifth largest in the world: 1,200 megawatts of installed capacity across roughly a thousand hectares in the municipality of Santiago do Cacém, near Sines. Construction involves felling around 1.5 million trees, mostly eucalyptus.

The ProtegeAlentejo association argues the Environmental Impact Statement was approved despite unfavourable opinions from the Institute for Nature Conservation and Forests, the National Laboratory of Energy and Geology, and even the agency's own head of environmental impact assessment. In January 2024, the Public Prosecutor's Office filed a case at the Administrative and Fiscal Court of Beja against the agency's licence, arguing it disregarded a wide range of land-use and natural resource protection rules.

More than two years on, the case still awaits a first-instance ruling, even though the agency has appealed. Construction, which Iberdrola once expected to finish by 2025, remains suspended by the courts.
