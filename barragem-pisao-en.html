No. 42 ⚖ In court Dam

Court suspends work on the Pisão dam in Crato for the third time

Crato, Portalegre district, Alto Alentejo

⚖ Works suspended by the courts since May · Castelo Branco court hearing the parties since 26 October; the judge will rule on the validity of the EIS · Government extends the works to 2030

A dam costing over €220 million that would submerge the village of Pisão. Four environmental associations have contested the project's public interest in court since 2022; public water supply would account for just over 1% of the reservoir's annual volume, against 65% for agricultural use. Works began in September 2025 and have been suspended and resumed several times; the Southern Central Administrative Court confirmed a new suspension in May 2026. Since 26 October, the Castelo Branco court has been hearing the parties before ruling on the validity of the EIS.

What is at stake

The Pisão Dam, in the municipality of Crato (Alto Alentejo), budgeted at about 222 million euros, would submerge the village of Pisão and requires felling tens of thousands of holm and cork oaks.

Cork and holm oak woodland. The works clear 680 hectares of mature holm and cork oak forest in good condition, more than 40,000 trees. The environmental impact study acknowledges damage to natural habitats of Community interest, one of them a priority, mostly montado and grassland steppe.

The Seda stream and its fish. The dam turns the Seda stream from flowing to still water and changes flows all the way to its mouth. Of the 13 fish species found, six are native: southern Iberian chub, Endangered; calandino, Vulnerable; Steindachner's barbel, Near Threatened; Iberian barbel, Iberian nase and southern Iberian spined loach, Least Concern. Quercus warns that some may disappear there and that the reservoir favours invasive exotic fish.

The birds. The study confirmed 126 bird species, 29 of them with a threat category in the Red Book. Confirmed threatened species include goshawk, golden eagle, Bonelli's eagle, Egyptian vulture, red kite (the area is a roost for 5% of the country's red kites), black stork, great bustard, Montagu's harrier, marsh harrier, stone-curlew, European nightjar, red-necked nightjar and great spotted cuckoo; Quercus also mentions eagle owl and cinereous vulture.

The birds of the plains. The irrigation block cuts across 289 hectares (21.9%) of the Alter do Chão Important Bird Area, a priority area for the little bustard. The little bustard lost about half its national population between 2006 and 2016 and has almost vanished outside IBAs and SPAs.

The people of Pisão. The reservoir floods the village of Pisão and forces its residents out. The NGOs point out that public supply would be little more than 1% of the water, and that the Póvoa e Meadas dam would be enough for it.
