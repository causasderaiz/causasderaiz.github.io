No. 171 ◐ Monitoring Very high voltage power line

Radiant Jupiter plans a 32.83 km, 400 kV line that may cross the Carregueira montado, in Chamusca, to connect two solar plants

Carregueira (Chamusca), Santa Margarida da Coutada (Constância) and Abrantes, up to the Abrantes switching station, in Pego

🗳 Public consultation until 24 November 2026 · Scoping Proposal no. 284 (APA)

🗳 Take part in the public consultation

Radiant Jupiter wants to connect the Casal da Valeira and Vale Pequeno solar plants, in Chamusca, to the national grid through a 400 kV line, 32.83 km long with 89 pylons, up to the Abrantes switching station. The first route was refused by the Army in 2023. The plants, approved with conditions, require felling 675 cork oaks. The scoping of the new study is in public consultation until 24 November.

What is at stake

In the 2023 consultation, GEOTA asked for an unfavourable decision on the plants and the line: it objected to the felling of 675 scattered cork oaks, the lack of alternative locations and the proximity of the Paul do Boquilobo Biosphere Reserve. RESIPINUS, the resin tappers' association, gave an unfavourable opinion over almost 400 hectares of deforestation, 65 of them stone pine. The Abrantes and Chamusca councils did not object to the route proposed at the time. The new consultation accepts submissions on participa.pt until 24 November 2026.

The cork oaks. The plants require felling 675 scattered cork oaks. APA found that panels and line pylons clash with cork oak stands, where the law forbids conversion (Decree-Law no. 169/2001). The cork oak is a protected species.

The box. Box (Buxus sempervirens), classified "Endangered" in the Red List of the Vascular Flora of Mainland Portugal, may occur along the last 10.4 km of the 2023 route; APA ordered a new survey.

The birds. APA considers bird collision and electrocution the most relevant ecological impact of the line. Added to the other lines in the region, it forms a network that causes exclusion, barrier effects and collision risk.

Those who live from the forest. The resin tappers of RESIPINUS count almost 400 hectares of deforestation, 65 of stone pine, on land ready for resin tapping; the offsets have no defined location.
