No. 35 ⚖ In court Intensive agriculture

Tangerines in Alcácer do Sal: six organisations take the orchard over a critical aquifer to court

Murta and Monte Novo estates, Alcácer do Sal, Alentejo coast

⚖ Lawsuit at the Beja Administrative and Tax Court (2 Nov. 2026) · groups' fundraiser · complaint to the European Commission

Donate: A nossa água tem limites (Dunas Livres e Água & Sal, ação judicial)

An intensive tangerine orchard on about 260 hectares in the Comporta/Galé Special Area of Conservation, owned by the Aquaterra group (US fund Davidson Kempner), was approved on its fourth attempt after four public consultations met with strong opposition. Its 12 boreholes can draw 1.74 million m³ of water a year, nearly double what Alcácer do Sal's public network supplied in 2023, and LNEG said the project should not be approved. Six organisations have taken the approval to the Beja Administrative and Tax Court to safeguard the aquifer and opened a fundraiser, "Our water has limits".

What is at stake

On the Murta and Monte Novo estates, in Santa Maria do Castelo and Comporta, Alcácer do Sal, Expoente Frugal, part of the Aquaterra group, linked to the US fund Davidson Kempner, wants to plant an intensive tangerine orchard irrigated with groundwater. The first three versions, with 722 and then 658 hectares of avocado, were rejected. The fourth, with tangerines, received a conditional favourable Environmental Impact Statement from CCDR Alentejo on 3 August 2026 and was later cut from 295 to about 260 hectares, with the same water volume: 12 boreholes up to 288 metres deep and up to 1.74 million m³ a year. The aquifer, the only water source for the people of Alcácer do Sal and Grândola, including Comporta, Carvalhal and Melides, loses more water than it receives, according to LNEG. Alcácer do Sal council gave an unfavourable opinion and all four public consultations met with strong opposition. Intensive farming already covers more than 5,000 hectares of the conservation area.

Water for Alcácer and Grândola. The aquifer south of the Sado is the only water source for the people of Alcácer do Sal and Grândola, including Comporta, Carvalhal and Melides. According to LNEG it loses more water than it receives, and the ground is already subsiding on the left bank of the Sado. The promoter's own study expects the water level to drop about 25 metres in August, and the Environmental Impact Statement itself rates the impact as high in magnitude, very significant and certain.

Dunes and pine woodland. The estate lies in the Comporta/Galé Special Area of Conservation, part of Natura 2000. About 88% of the study area is habitat of Community interest, including Juniperus navicularis scrub, priority habitat 2250*, and about 40% of the orchard would sit on habitat 2260, dunes with sclerophyllous vegetation. The Alcácer P'lo Ambiente movement counted 40,984 stone pines and 442 maritime pines to be felled. ICNF rated the impacts on these habitats as very significant.

Those already drawing on the aquifer. About 18 million m³ a year are already drawn nearby for other farms, public supply and industry. The orchard's 1.74 million m³ would be nearly double the 886,000 m³ Alcácer do Sal's public network supplied in 2023. According to the public consultation, local farmers are deepening their boreholes.
