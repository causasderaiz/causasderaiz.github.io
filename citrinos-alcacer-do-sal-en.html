Tangerines in Alcácer do Sal: Quercus files complaint with the European Commission

Murta and Monte Novo estates, Alcácer do Sal, Alentejo coast

⚡ Complaint filed with the European Commission

A 295-hectare tangerine project in the Comporta/Galé Special Conservation Area was approved on its fourth attempt, despite 99.2% of public consultation input rejecting it.

The Aquaterra group, linked to a North American investment fund, received a conditional Environmental Impact Statement on 3 August 2026 for an intensive tangerine farming project across roughly 295 hectares at the Murta and Monte Novo estates, in Alcácer do Sal. It is the fourth version of the project: the previous three, which proposed 722 hectares of avocado, were all rejected.

The site lies within the Natura 2000 network, in the Comporta/Galé Special Conservation Area, over an aquifer already in critical condition, with land subsidence and rising piezometric pressure. The project still depends on groundwater abstraction through 12 deep boreholes. Alentejo's regional environmental authority approved the statement despite an unfavourable opinion from Quercus and despite 99.2% of public consultation submissions rejecting the project; ZERO called the decision a "gross error".

Quercus will file a formal complaint with the European Commission for breach of EU law, calling for the protection of the Comporta-Galé conservation area and the region's aquifers, and is considering an injunction at Portugal's administrative courts to suspend the statement. The association is also demanding an independent opinion from the National Laboratory of Energy and Geology on the aquifer and the resumption of the National Water Council, which hasn't met in three years.
