How a wind farm ends up on your doorstep

One day a mast appears on the hill, then machines, then the towers. The early stages of a project like this leave almost no public trace. This is the path, step by step, and the signs to watch for.

1

Grid connection

The developer secures a grid capacity reservation title (Decree-Law 15/2022) or wins a state auction. The Vilar reservoir lot was awarded in 2022, four years before the public consultation.

Sign

Almost none. This is the most hidden stage.

2

The land

The developer negotiates with landowners and common land (baldios).

Sign

Neighbours receiving lease offers.

3

Measurements

For a wind farm, a mast goes up to measure the wind. In Estremoz, the Municipal Assembly rejected the Serra d'Ossa masts on 25 September 2026.

Sign

A tall mast with instruments on top; the item on the Municipal Assembly agenda.

4

Acceleration zones

Since 28 August 2026 there are more than 800 acceleration zones for wind and solar (Council of Ministers Resolution 172-A/2026). Projects that fall within them are exempt from an environmental impact study.

Sign

Check whether your parish is on the zones map.

5

Environmental impact assessment

Only projects above certain sizes or in sensitive areas go through it; the 2023 Environmental Simplex (Decree-Law 11/2023) exempted many. When it applies, the study goes to public consultation for 30 working days, and the APA or the CCDR decides: favourable, conditional or unfavourable.

Sign

A notice at the parish and town hall; the consultation on participa.pt. This is the moment to take part.

6

Licences

The council gives its opinion and licenses the planning side. Felling cork and holm oaks needs a government order: 755 were authorised in Penamacor. The production licence comes from the DGEG.

Sign

Licence applications and notices at the town hall.

7

Construction and operation

Roads, trenches, platforms and power lines. Then about 25 years of operation.

Sign

Machinery, test drilling and markings on the ground.

How to stay alert

  • Check participa.pt and the notices of the parish and town hall.
  • Read the agendas and minutes of the Council and the Municipal Assembly.
  • Ask the council for the documents: it is your right (Law 26/2016).
  • Watch the land: masts, test drilling, markings, lease offers.
  • Follow the map and the Conflitos Ambientais WhatsApp channel.
