No. 155 ◐ Monitoring Super-intensive olive grove

Night olive harvesting: machines suck up the birds sleeping in the olive trees

Super-intensive olive groves of the Alentejo

◐ Banned since 2020 · harvest from November to January, inspected by the ICNF

In super-intensive olive groves, harvesting machines working at night shake and suck up the birds sleeping in the trees. In two GNR night inspections in the Alentejo, in 2018 and 2019, 375 dead birds were found; in an inspection in Avis, the ICNF counted 6.4 dead birds per hectare. Night harvesting has been banned since 2020, and disturbing or killing birds is an administrative offence and a crime.

What is at stake

An INIAV study during the 2019/2020 harvest confirmed that mechanical night harvesting kills birds significantly and that measures to scare them away do not work: the machines' floodlights disorient them and stop them escaping. In July 2020 the ICNF began sanctioning the practice and the four sector associations (CAP, Confragri, Casa do Azeite and Olivum) suspended night harvesting. In 2023 the ICNF kept the ban for the third year. The harvest runs from November to the end of January.

The birds. 17 species were identified, mostly migratory and protected: song thrush, goldfinch, greenfinch, chaffinch, serin, siskin, Sardinian warbler and Iberian chiffchaff. Dormice and wall lizards also die.

The numbers. In Avis, the ICNF found at least 480 dead birds in 25 loads of olives harvested from 75 hectares. Applied to the 15,000 hectares of super-intensive groves, the average gives more than 96,000 birds a year; Quercus estimates 70,000 to 100,000.

The bats. A study of olive farming intensification in Portugal shows that the number of bat and bird species falls from traditional to super-intensive groves.
