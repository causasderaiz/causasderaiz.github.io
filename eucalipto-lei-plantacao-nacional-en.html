Eucalyptus: industry wants to loosen the planting law, environmentalists call for a moratorium

National: mainland Portugal

⚡ Navigator calls for a review of the afforestation rules; Quercus and Floresta do Futuro demand a halt to eucalyptus

Since 2017, the law only allows eucalyptus to be planted where eucalyptus already grew. In 2026, the paper company Navigator asked for the afforestation rules to stop being "prohibitive and punitive", and the public Floresta Ativa programme began funding eucalyptus plantations too. Environmental movements and associations link eucalyptus monocultures to the major wildfires and are demanding a moratorium on new plantations and a reduction of eucalyptus area by 2030.

After the 2017 wildfires, the law banned new eucalyptus plantations, allowing only the replanting of areas previously occupied by the species. According to the National Forest Inventory (2015 data), cited by Quercus, eucalyptus covers about 844,000 hectares, close to 26% of the mainland's forest area.

On 13 January 2026, Quercus warned that more than two thirds of burned eucalyptus plantations are hidden from national statistics, because young, regenerating or recently felled eucalyptus stands are classified as "scrubland" in satellite data. It gave the 2024 Arouca fire and the Pedrógão Grande fire as examples, and called for the classification protocols to be corrected, for eucalyptus plantations to require a licence, and for them to be georeferenced on public platforms.

On 22 May 2026, Navigator's chief executive, João Lé, called for a review of the legislation limiting eucalyptus planting, so that "national regulation moves from a purely prohibitive and punitive paradigm to a model that fosters and encourages investment". "Portugal urgently needs an RJAR [Legal Regime for Afforestation and Reforestation] that is an instrument of promotion and not of blockage", he said, arguing for the afforestation of scrubland with productive potential.

On 29 July 2026, according to Lusa, the Floresta do Futuro (Forest of the Future) movement accused the Government of funding eucalyptus plantations while Europe burns: under the new rules of the Floresta Ativa programme, announced by the Secretary of State for Forests, Rui Ladeira, and relaunched with four million euros from the Environmental Fund, all species, including eucalyptus, can now apply, and eucalyptus plantations can receive up to 800 euros per hectare. The movement says that, at the same time, those who planted native species struggle to recover the money invested in fuel management.

On 20 August 2026, Quercus and the international Environmental Paper Network demanded a "moratorium on new afforestation and reforestation with eucalyptus", binding targets to reduce eucalyptus area by 2030, more inspections, a tax on the pulp and biomass industries, and gradual replacement with native species such as oaks, cork oaks, chestnuts and holm oaks.
