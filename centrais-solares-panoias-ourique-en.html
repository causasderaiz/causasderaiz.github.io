Galp in Ourique: ZERO opposes two solar plants

Galp and Bartolomeu Dias Renewables plan two solar plants with more than a million panels in the Campo Branco, next to the Castro Verde Special Protection Area, in Ourique

Panóias and Conceição, Ourique (Campo Branco), Beja district

⚡ Environmental impact statements extended in 2026 · ZERO opposed both plants

In Panóias and Conceição, Ourique, Galp wants to add Ourique II, with 239,904 panels on 248 hectares, to the Ourique I plant, and Bartolomeu Dias Renewables a plant of 840,840 panels on an area of 441 hectares, on cereal fields and pasture next to the Castro Verde Special Protection Area, home to steppe birds. ZERO opposed both, and the 2022 environmental impact statements were extended in 2026.

What is at stake

Both plants lie in the Campo Branco of Castro Verde, an open landscape of cereal fields, pasture and fallow, the preferred habitat of steppe birds. In the 2022 consultation, ZERO asked for a buffer next to the SPA and warned that the cumulative effect of the region's many solar plants on migratory and threatened species could be very significant. Three local women opposed Ourique II because of the loss of tree cover and its closeness to inhabited areas.

The steppe birds. The Castro Verde SPA was designated for its steppe bird populations. The APA notes that the plants lie in open, favourable habitat for Montagu's harrier, little bustard, great bustard, stone-curlew, black-bellied sandgrouse, European roller and common crane. The Ourique II study itself rates the loss of habitat for steppe species as a negative, permanent and significant impact.

The fields, the pines and the oaks. Ourique II takes up rain-fed and irrigated farmland. According to Centro PINUS and ZERO, Bartolomeu Dias would mean cutting about 40 hectares of stone pine and several cork and holm oaks, in an area that includes National Agricultural Reserve.

The people of Panóias and Conceição. Turismo de Portugal counts five holiday rentals within 3 km, in the villages of Conceição and Alcarias, on which the plant has a high and a low visual intrusion respectively. Both plants add to Ourique I and to new projects that, according to the APA, may increase the significance of cumulative impacts.
