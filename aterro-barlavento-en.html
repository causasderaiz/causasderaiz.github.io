Barlavento Landfill: residents protest against smell, flies and expansion

Porto de Lagos, Portimão municipality, bordering Silves

⚡ Residents protest against smell and flies · Algar wants to expand with new Cell E · ZERO accuses it of breaching organic waste law

The Barlavento Sanitary Landfill, in Porto de Lagos, Portimão municipality, is close to maximum capacity and causing unbearable smell and swarms of flies in Odelouca, Porto de Lagos and Rasmalho. On 12 March 2026, the Associação de Moradores de Odelouca, Porto de Lagos e Rasmalho (residents' association) organised a protest outside the landfill, covered by national broadcaster RTP and the local press. Instead of considering closure, Algar, the company that runs the multi-municipal waste system for the Algarve's 16 municipalities, plans to expand the site with a new cell (Cell E, around 98,900 m², capacity for 150,000 tonnes a year). In November 2025, environmental association ZERO accused Algar of continuing to dump untreated organic waste, around 40% of what enters the landfill, a practice banned under Decree-Law 24/2024; according to Algar's own environmental impact study, the landfill is responsible for 55% of greenhouse gas emissions in Portimão municipality. In 2024, Algar also tried to expropriate land in Silves for the expansion, but Silves Municipal Council unanimously opposed it, echoing the popular opposition that had already blocked a similar attempt in 1995.

The Barlavento Sanitary Landfill is located in Porto de Lagos, Portimão municipality, bordering Silves, and is run by Algar, the company that manages urban waste for the Algarve's 16 municipalities. The site is close to maximum capacity, and residents of Odelouca, Porto de Lagos and Rasmalho have lived for months with unbearable smell and swarms of flies.

On 12 March 2026, the Associação de Moradores de Odelouca, Porto de Lagos e Rasmalho organised a protest outside the landfill, covered by national broadcaster RTP and the local press. One resident told the Portugal Resident that her kitchen floor and counters were completely covered with flies, and that “it becomes impossible to cook”. Environmentalist António Lambe, from Ação Ambiental para o Barlavento, who has followed Algarve landfill projects since the 1990s, said the problem is not an isolated case and stressed the importance of educating younger generations about the impacts of poor waste management.

Instead of considering closure, Algar plans to expand the site with a new cell, Cell E, covering around 98,900 m² with capacity for 150,000 tonnes of waste a year. In November 2025, ZERO accused Algar of breaching the law by continuing to dump untreated organic waste, around 40% of what enters the landfill, a practice banned under Decree-Law 24/2024. According to ZERO, Algar's own environmental impact study admits the landfill is responsible for 55% of greenhouse gas emissions in Portimão municipality.

In 2024, Algar had already tried to expropriate land in Silves to expand the landfill. Silves Municipal Council unanimously opposed the move, backed by PSD and PS councillors, recalling that it had also unanimously opposed a similar attempt in 1995, at the time accompanied by strong popular mobilisation.
