No. 21 ⚡ Active situation Cross-border mining

Tungsten mine in A Gudiña threatens the Rabaçal river and Montesinho Natural Park

A Gudiña, Ourense, Galicia (Spain), 2 km from Vinhais, Bragança

◐ Mining temporarily suspended since 28 September 2026, on the orders of A Gudiña council · No cross-border assessment

An open-pit tungsten mine 2 km from the border moved forward without the cross-border impact assessment required by law, threatening the water supply of 14 Portuguese villages. In September 2026, A Gudiña council ordered the activity to stop and the company began a temporary suspension of the works.

Just a few kilometres from the Portuguese border, in Pentes, in the municipality of A Gudiña, in the Galician province of Ourense, the company Tungsten San Juan, a subsidiary of Sweden's Eurobattery Minerals, is preparing an open-pit tungsten mine, with production expected in the fourth quarter of 2026 and reserves estimated at 60,000 tonnes. The mine sits about 100 metres from the Ribeira de Pente stream, which flows into the Rabaçal river in Vinhais, Bragança district, supplying water to fourteen Portuguese villages inside Montesinho Natural Park.

Movimento UIVO, based in Vinhais, says the cross-border environmental impact assessment required under the Espoo Convention has never been carried out, even though the European Commission confirmed the obligation in July 2026. On 23 July, around 40 Portuguese and Galician environmentalists and local officials, including members of Ecoloxistas en Acción, gathered outside the church of San Martiño in A Gudiña to demand the assessment and delivered a letter to the Spanish municipality's mayor.

Meanwhile, a court in Santiago de Compostela opened an investigation into the 30-year extension of the mining concession, following a complaint from Ecoloxistas en Acción. Parish officials in Vinhais say residents have stopped drinking tap water for fear of contamination, and the Portuguese government admits it has never been consulted by Spain about the project's impacts on national territory.

The Pente stream flows into the Rabaçal river, inside Montesinho Natural Park. According to the ICNF's description of the park, accessed on 27 September 2026, Montesinho Natural Park is home to around 250 vertebrate species and 70% of the land mammal species found in Portugal, around 10% of which are listed as threatened in the Portuguese Red Book of Vertebrates. It holds one of the most important Iberian wolf populations and the only viable populations of freshwater pearl mussel (Margaritifera margaritifera) known in Portugal, and is part of the "Montesinho-Nogueira" Site of Community Importance and Special Protection Area of the Natura 2000 network.

On 24 September 2026, A Gudiña council ordered the immediate stoppage of activity at the Eurobattery Minerals mine until the company submits the required documentation; according to Galiciapress, there were complaints that the mine was operating without a planning licence or a valid environmental authorisation. On 28 September, the company announced that its Spanish subsidiary, Tungsten San Juan, had begun the administrative procedure for a temporary suspension of mining activity. According to Mensageiro de Bragança, the Spanish Supreme Court confirmed in January 2026 the conviction of Eurobattery Minerals' chief executive, Roberto García Martínez, to a two-year suspended prison sentence for aggravated fraud in an investment scheme involving gold mines in Sierra Leone. The Uivo Movement argues that the conditions are now in place for the mine to be definitively revoked.
