Waste incinerator in the Algarve: Government moves ahead despite environmentalist opposition

Algarve (site to be defined, to address the exhaustion of the Portimão and Loulé landfills)

⚡ Quercus and Zero object · APA tender expected 15 October

Environment and Energy Minister Maria da Graça Carvalho announced on 8 August the construction of a waste-to-energy plant in the Algarve, with investment of up to €300 million and start-up expected in 2034, to address the exhaustion of the Portimão and Loulé landfills, projected for around 2027. The Algarve produces more waste per capita than any other region in the country (400,000 tonnes/year, 85% landfilled), driven largely by seasonal tourism. Quercus calls incineration "completely anachronistic", incompatible with the PERSU 2030 targets and the country's climate commitments (the technology emits more CO₂ per kWh than fossil fuels), and demands the Government publish the alternatives study it commissioned from consultancy Ramboll; the Portuguese Environment Agency will launch a tender on 15 October to study technology and siting.

Environment and Energy Minister Maria da Graça Carvalho announced on 8 August 2026 that the Portuguese Environment Agency will move ahead with an energy-from-waste incineration unit in the Algarve, with investment of up to 300 million euros and operation expected to begin in 2034. The plan responds to the looming exhaustion of the Portimão and Loulé landfills, expected around 2027, in a region that produces 400,000 tonnes of urban waste a year, 85% of which goes to landfill.

Environmental group Zero called the proposal "harmful management" of the public interest and requested an urgent meeting with the minister to clarify the region's waste strategy. Quercus considers incineration "completely anachronistic", incompatible with PERSU 2030 targets and Portugal's climate commitments, arguing the technology emits more CO₂ per kWh than fossil fuels.

On 3 September, Quercus announced it will lead a White Paper on Waste in Portugal, with input from universities, municipalities and other bodies, to propose alternatives to incineration. The Environment Agency plans to launch a tender on 15 October to study the technology and final location of the unit.
