Carrazeda de Ansiães wildfire: residents challenge how the fire was fought

Vilarinho da Castanheira and Pinhal do Douro, Carrazeda de Ansiães, and Torre de Moncorvo, Bragança district, Trás-os-Montes

⚡ Petition to the Minister of Internal Administration; ANEPC is analysing the fire's timeline

Residents of Vilarinho da Castanheira and Pinhal do Douro set up the "Isto Não Fica Assim" ("This Won't Stand") movement after the August 2026 wildfire, which spread from Torre de Moncorvo and burned cork oaks, olive and almond trees and vineyards. They accuse the Douro sub-regional Civil Protection command of not knowing the territory, are seeking compensation and prevention measures, and are gathering signatures for a petition to the Minister of Internal Administration.

According to the Lusa news agency, the fire broke out on 15 August 2026 in the municipality of Torre de Moncorvo and spread the next day to Carrazeda de Ansiães, where it burned for several days and consumed about 4,000 hectares of scrub and farmland. JN describes a fire that lasted five days, starting in Cabeça Boa, and left "at least three thousand hectares burned, and heavy agricultural losses". On 10 September, the Secretary of State for Civil Protection, Rui Rocha, gave an estimate of more than 3,600 hectares burned across the two municipalities, a figure still to be confirmed.

On 26 August, JN reported that residents of Vilarinho da Castanheira and Pinhal do Douro, in Carrazeda de Ansiães, were calling for accountability, circulating a petition to send with a complaint to the Ministry of Internal Administration, and organising a protest against the "lack of coordination" of resources in fighting the fire.

On 30 August, the spokesperson for the "Isto Não Fica Assim" movement, Pedro Correia, told Lusa that residents are "outraged and indignant" with the National Emergency and Civil Protection Authority (ANEPC), which they believe "failed" because it did not know the area. He said that on the night of 15 to 16 August he told the command that resources had been withdrawn from a fire front near the village's water mills, and that his suggestion was ignored; the area around the mills burned. He criticised the Douro sub-regional command for the resources it deployed and for what he sees as a "let it burn" policy, but stressed: "We have nothing to say against the firefighters. The firefighters were the ones who kept the tragedy from being even bigger". "The land was almost entirely burned, we lost our cork oaks, we lost many olive trees, almond trees, vines", he said. The movement wants to be compensated "for the damage caused to us by the command's incompetence" and is calling for prevention measures such as firebreaks and planting cork oaks and more resistant trees. It is running a petition, online and on paper, to deliver to the Minister of Internal Administration, and threatened to hold a protest if the Government did not listen.

On 10 September, after a meeting with local mayors, Rui Rocha said ANEPC is analysing the timeline of the fire, which was fought between 16 and 19 August: "It is legitimate for a group of citizens to protest and to bring us their concerns about this fire". He said he was waiting for the petition to be delivered and that an assessment would be made to understand "whether anything did not go well, or went really badly".
