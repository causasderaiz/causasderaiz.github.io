No. 81 ◐ Monitoring Land-use planning

Vila Franca de Xira wants to triple the Lisbon-North Logistics Platform

Castanheira do Ribatejo, Vila Franca de Xira

◐ Public consultation closed 4 September · Awaiting strategic environmental assessment

Vila Franca de Xira City Council approved by majority a detailed plan expanding the Lisbon-North Logistics Platform by around 340 hectares, roughly 300 of them for logistics, technology and data centres. The CDU (Communist coalition) criticised converting about 295 hectares of farmland and ecologically valuable land, estimating €491 million in land-value gains for owners and developers; PSD, IL and Chega raised concerns about the power grid and firefighting capacity. The public consultation closed on 4 September with no outcome announced yet; the council promises a strategic environmental assessment before proceeding.

Vila Franca de Xira City Council approved, on 25 August 2026, moving ahead with a detailed plan that expands the Lisbon-North Logistics Platform in Castanheira do Ribatejo by around 340 hectares, nearly triple the current area of about 100 hectares, occupied since 2008. Around 300 hectares are earmarked for economic activities (logistics, technology and data centres), with the area closest to the Tejo river becoming a riverside urban park. The process was in public consultation until 4 September 2026.

The CDU (Communist coalition) criticised converting about 295 hectares of farmland and ecologically valuable land, estimating €491 million in land-value gains for owners and developers from the reclassification. PSD and IL called for stronger guarantees on the power grid, needed for the planned data centres, and Chega demanded assurances on infrastructure and firefighting capacity. Mayor Fernando Paulo Ferreira argues the project will create around 5,000 jobs and promises a strategic environmental assessment before proceeding. In an earlier phase of the same project (2021-2022), Quercus and the civic movement XIRADANIA had already issued a negative opinion on the environmental impact study for a roughly 38-hectare subdivision on the same platform, citing insufficient study of alternative locations and downplaying of contamination risk to the country's largest freshwater aquifer.
