Conflict

Felling of cork and holm oaks

The cork oak and the holm oak are protected by law. Stands can only be cleared when the Government declares a project to be of essential public interest. These are those declarations, and the conflicts on the map where these trees come down.

What the law says

Decree-Law no. 169/2001 ↗ prohibits converting cork and holm oak stands, except for projects of essential public interest or of significant interest to the local economy. Felling any tree, in a stand or on its own, requires authorisation.

The declaration is issued by the ministers responsible for forests and for the project. The developer must show the project's economic and social interest and that there is no valid alternative location. As compensation, it must plant or improve an area no smaller than 1.25 times the area felled, on its own land.

Montados (habitat 6310), cork oak woods (9330) and holm oak woods (9340) are habitats of community interest ↗ under the Habitats Directive. The cork oak has been Portugal's national tree since Resolution no. 15/2012 of the Assembly of the Republic.
