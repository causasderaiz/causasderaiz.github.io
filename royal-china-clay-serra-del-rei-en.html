No. 64 ⚡ Active situation Kaolin mining

Motamineral plans a 51.4-hectare kaolin mine next to Serra d'El-Rei, Peniche, and 12 municipalities ask for an unfavourable environmental decision

Serra d'El-Rei, Peniche, Leiria district

⚡ OesteCIM (12 municipalities) asks APA for an unfavourable environmental impact declaration (11 Sep 2026)

The C-185 “Royal China Clay” mining concession, held by Motamineral – Minerais Industriais, would exploit 51.4 hectares of kaolinitic sands across three cores near Serra d'El-Rei (Peniche), with an on-site industrial washing plant. The public consultation closed on 1 September with 7,368 submissions; the councils of Óbidos and Peniche gave an unfavourable opinion on the project, and Quercus joined the call to reject it, warning of cumulative impacts on the region.

Mining concession C-185 "Royal China Clay", awarded to Motamineral – Minerais Industriais, S.A., aims to extract around 101 hectares of kaolinitic sand and clay across three sites near Serra d'El-Rei, in the municipality of Peniche, with an on-site washing plant. The extracted material is destined for the ceramic-paste factory of MCS – Mota Ceramic Solutions. Projected annual output is around 569,100 tonnes (31,800 tonnes of kaolin, 270,000 of washed sand and 267,300 of common clay), under a two-decade state concession.

The public consultation on the environmental impact study ran from 22 July to 1 September 2026. According to Quercus, the concession would affect 471 of the 662 cork oaks in the area, with compensation planned for just 300 new specimens (which the group calls "derisory") as well as nine vulnerable animal species, a wet-heathland habitat and the native plant Drosophyllum lusitanicum.

The Óbidos and Peniche municipal councils, the Atouguia da Baleia, Peniche and Serra d'El-Rei parish councils, Quercus and the Portuguese Association of Landscape Architects all issued unfavourable opinions on the project. The Left Bloc questioned the government about the process on 3 September.

On 11 September 2026, the council of the Oeste Intermunicipal Community (OesteCIM), grouping 12 municipalities in the region, decided to formally oppose the project and asked the Portuguese Environment Agency for an unfavourable Environmental Impact Declaration, calling it "incompatible with the environmental and territorial support capacity of the surrounding area". OesteCIM points to errors in the impact study regarding distances to nearby homes (the study states 4 km, when houses actually stand 20, 150 and 450 metres from the site), and warns of risk to 24 groundwater catchments and the São Domingos reservoir, near which a sand-washing plant is planned. The council also estimates the mine would create only 12 direct jobs, against the environmental and traffic costs (11 trucks per hour projected) for the region. Peniche's mayor announced he would request meetings with the Environment Minister and APA.

Excavations could reach 46 metres deep and the operation would last twenty and a half years, in the São Domingos stream basin, whose reservoir supplies Peniche. Quercus also points to the lack of monitoring of respirable crystalline silica, a dust linked to silicosis and lung cancer. On 28 October 2026, Jornal Maio asked: "If the local councils are against it, why are their concerns not decisive in this discussion?"

On 9 November 2026, residents accused Motamineral of producing an environmental impact study with gross errors that ignores homes a few metres from the concession; the project is under review by the APA (Correio da Manhã).
