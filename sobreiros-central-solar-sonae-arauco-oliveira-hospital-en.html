No. 186 ◐ Monitoring Solar plant and cork oak felling

Political parties oppose the felling of 508 cork oaks for Sonae Arauco's solar plant in Oliveira do Hospital

São Paio de Gramaços, Oliveira do Hospital, Coimbra district

◐ Felling authorised by the Government (Order no. 552/2026, 19 January 2026) · Questions to the Government from BE and PCP

In January 2026 the Government declared the 13.3 MW self-consumption solar plant for Sonae Arauco's wood-panel factory in São Paio de Gramaços, Oliveira do Hospital, to be of essential public interest, and authorised the felling of 508 cork oaks, 172 mature and 336 young, over 2.8 hectares. The Left Bloc and the Communist Party asked the Government why it allowed this, after PSD/CDS-PP councillors voted in 2025 against the first version of the project, on the slopes of the Alva valley. As compensation, the company will plant cork oaks on 6.35 hectares, in Freixiosa parish, Mangualde, according to the Government.

What is at stake

Cork oaks may only be felled with a declaration of essential public interest and compensation planting, which the law sets at no less than 1.25 times the affected area. The Directorate-General for Energy and Geology waived an environmental impact assessment and CCDR Centro issued a favourable opinion. According to ICNF, the plant does not touch any protected areas. Between December 2025 and January 2026 the Government authorised the felling of 9,464 protected trees, mostly cork oaks, for projects across the country.

The cork oak stand. 172 mature and 336 young cork oaks are to go, a species protected by law. According to the Left Bloc, compensation planting does not restore the ecological value of an established stand, and there is land available less than five kilometres from the factory.

Wildfire defence. The land lies in a priority fire-prevention area with high rural fire hazard, according to the council. The Left Bloc and the PSD/CDS-PP councillors object to the loss of forest tracks they say are public and used in firefighting; the Left Bloc calls the cork oak a "firefighter tree".

The Alva valley landscape. The PSD/CDS-PP councillors called the original project, 12,662 panels on the slopes of the Alva valley, a "serious attack on the landscape" with a negative visual impact on one of the region's main tourist areas, and accused the council of not looking for alternative sites.
