Algarve desalination plant: works proceed in Albufeira amid new legal challenge

Praia da Rocha Baixinha / Olhos de Água, Albufeira

⚖ Works proceed · New injunction filed

Over 100 million euros to desalinate seawater in the Algarve. Already the target of two injunctions, still contested by environmentalists and fishers.

The Algarve Seawater Desalination Plant, under construction next to Praia da Rocha Baixinha in Albufeira, represents an investment of over €100 million, financed through the Recovery and Resilience Plan. The Águas do Algarve project aims to convert an initial 16 million cubic metres of seawater into drinking water per year, potentially rising to 24 million.

The project has already faced two court injunctions. In 2025, associations linked to the Sustainable Water Platform, including Almargem and A Rocha Portugal, unsuccessfully challenged the length of the public consultation on the Execution Project's Environmental Compliance Report. In April 2026, with construction already under way, Seacliff, the former owner of the expropriated land where the plant is being built, filed a new injunction at the Administrative and Fiscal Court of Loulé, with immediate suspensive effect on construction.

Fishermen from Albufeira and Quarteira, together with Albufeira's mayor, joined a protest at Praia da Rocha Baixinha, calling for a fuller assessment of the impacts on the sea and local fishing. Despite the opposition, the Environment Minister insists the project will proceed, and Águas do Algarve expects to complete the plant within about two years.

On 3 April 2024 APA issued a conditionally favourable Environmental Impact Statement. The Plataforma Água Sustentável challenged it on 8 April for leaving the assessment of impacts on marine life, fishing, noise and brine discharge to a later stage. In the RECAPE consultation, from 3 to 23 July 2025, the platform reaffirmed its opposition, noting that the first consultation had 214 submissions and that the brine can reach 70,000 mg/l of salt.

In May 2026, in the public consultation on the licence to occupy the seabed with the intake pipes and the brine outfall, Plataforma Água Sustentável asked how APA could approve the whole project, with 38 conditions, when this part, without which the desalination plant cannot work, had not yet been assessed. It also noted that the decision on the detailed design still requires 20 missing documents and 123 mitigation measures.
