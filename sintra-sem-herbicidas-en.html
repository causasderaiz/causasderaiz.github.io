Sintra sem Herbicidas: against glyphosate use in public spaces

Sintra municipality

⚡ Petition archived with no change to the law; movement launches mailing campaign to public bodies (Sep 2026)

Sintra sem Herbicidas is a citizens' movement active since 2015 campaigning to end the use of herbicides, glyphosate in particular, for weed control in public spaces across Sintra municipality. It filed a petition to the Portuguese Parliament for a ban on dangerous herbicides in urban public spaces, which was heard by the Agriculture and Fisheries Committee, and secured a motion passed by the Colares parish assembly in April 2026 demanding an end to glyphosate use. The group has documented and reported to Sintra Council and to GNR-SEPNA cases of herbicide applied near watercourses and schools, even after the council publicly committed to stopping the practice, and took part in public consultations on the national action plans for pollinators and for the sustainable use of pesticides.

The grassroots movement Sintra sem Herbicidas (Sintra Without Herbicides) denounces the use of glyphosate and other herbicides in public spaces across the municipality of Sintra, in a fight that began in 2015.

That year the Municipal Council committed to a phased reduction plan for glyphosate and, in 2019, announced it had eliminated it from public space, replacing it with a biological herbicide based on pelargonic acid. In March 2019, however, Quercus had reported non-compliance complaints to the GNR (national guard) across several municipalities in the region, describing the situation as “particularly serious in the municipality of Sintra”. The movement disputes that this has been consistently upheld across all of the municipality's parishes, and continues to document and report the use of chemical herbicides.

The national petition "For the prohibition of the use of dangerous herbicides in urban public spaces in Portugal", filed by Tiago Filipe Cavaco Santos, was admitted to the Portuguese Parliament as Petition 102/XVII/1.ª, gathered 708 signatures, and had MP Irene Costa named as rapporteur in February 2026. In June 2026, the petitioner was heard by the Agriculture and Fisheries Committee, accompanied by Quercus's president, before the Chega, PS and PSD parliamentary groups; five supporting opinions backing the petition were also submitted. On the Parliament's own portal, the petition is now listed as "archived".

Given the position taken by the parties present at the hearing and the silence of those absent, and after the petition's final report, Sintra sem Herbicidas says it has little or no hope that the law will change in favour of a ban, and describes the outcome as favourable to the agrochemical industry and contrary to the Precautionary Principle and to instruments already approved, such as Portugal's Action Plan for the Conservation and Sustainability of Pollinators. In response, the movement launched a "mass mailing" campaign in September 2026 targeting the parliamentary groups, the President of the Assembly of the Republic, the President of the Republic, the Ministry of Environment, the Directorate-General for Food and Veterinary Affairs (DGAV), the National Association of Portuguese Municipalities (ANMP) and the National Association of Parishes (ANAFRE), to press these bodies not to let the issue drop. The movement notes that concern over herbicide use in public spaces is reflected in dozens of petitions nationwide and in Quercus's "Municipalities Without Glyphosate/Herbicides" campaign, already joined by 80 municipalities.

We have not yet found independent press coverage of the motion passed by the Colares parish council or of the new mailing campaign; these two elements are based on the movement's own account.
