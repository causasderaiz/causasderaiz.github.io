Pollution of the Lis river basin: untreated sewage discharges, no lasting solution in sight

Lis river basin, Leiria and Marinha Grande municipalities

⚡ Claimants seek €256,000 in damages after a 2025 discharge of up to 25,000 m³ of raw sewage; Oikos confirms a pollution hotspot on the Lena river in 2026

In August 2025, a breakdown at the Monte Real pumping station, run by Águas do Centro Litoral, forced a discharge of between 20,000 and 25,000 m³ of raw sewage into the Lis river, with swimming banned at Vieira Beach; in August 2026, 12 claimants are seeking €256,000 in damages, and the Oikos association confirmed, in September 2026 tests, a pollution hotspot on the Lena river, a Lis tributary, in urban Leiria.

On 12 August 2025, a breakdown at the Monte Real pumping station in Leiria municipality, run by the company Águas do Centro Litoral (AdCL), forced the discharge of thousands of cubic metres of untreated effluent into the Lis river, which flows out at Vieira Beach in Marinha Grande municipality, and into irrigation channels. The Portuguese Environment Agency estimated that between 20,000 and 25,000 m³ of raw sewage were released into the river. At the time, swimming was banned at Vieira Beach for three days, and Leiria Municipality advised against irrigation, swimming and fishing in the stretch of river downstream of Monte Real.

Marinha Grande City Council set up a support service for those affected by the discharge. In August 2026, AdCL confirmed it had received 12 claims from potential claimants, totalling around €256,000, with individual amounts ranging from €400 to €160,000, covering mostly agriculture, fishing, catering and retail. The compensation process is being handled by AdCL's insurer, with no payment made as of the report's date.

AdCL says it has, since the breakdown, reinforced the ultraviolet disinfection system at the Coimbrão wastewater treatment plant and repaired the pumping system at the Monte Real station, and has under way a project to redirect effluent from the Vieira de Leiria plant to Coimbrão for treatment, plus an investment of around €1 million to reinforce the resilience of four pumping stations after storm damage in early 2026. A remodelling of the Coimbrão plant is also planned, with an estimated investment of €32 million.

In September 2026, the environmental association Oikos, as part of the "Lis River Basin: Acting to Know, Protect and Value" project, collected water samples at 18 points across the river basin in June 2026, together with the Leiria Local Health Unit's Health Authority. Results remained within the historical range for most points, but the association flagged a pollution hotspot with "concerning" levels on the Lena river, a Lis tributary, in the Parque Verde area of Leiria.
