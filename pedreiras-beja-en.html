Bairro das Pedreiras: Roma community has lived without water or sanitation for two decades in Beja

Bairro das Pedreiras, Beja, Baixo Alentejo

⚡ No structural response · Council carried out a new survey in 2025

About a thousand people, over 200 children among them, have lived since 2005 in a neighbourhood without piped water or basic sanitation, next to a kennel and Beja City Council's materials yard.

Bairro das Pedreiras was opened in January 2006 by Beja City Council to rehouse about 50 families, roughly two hundred people, who had been living in shacks in the Bairro da Esperança. What was meant to be a temporary solution became permanent: two decades on, dozens of self-built shacks have sprung up around the original brick houses, without piped water, sanitation or electricity.

According to Público, about a thousand people, over 200 children among them, live in conditions the newspaper describes as "sub-human": no drinking water, accumulated rubbish, dry scrub where snakes and rats breed, and temperatures above 38°C in summer with no relief. The neighbourhood sits next to the council's kennel and materials yard: the animal shelter's facilities were renovated while the families remain in shacks.

Beja City Council carried out a new survey of the neighbourhood in 2025, but residents and journalists point to two decades of neglect by successive local and national governments. The Portuguese press describes the case as anticiganismo (anti-Roma discrimination). Internationally, the same pattern, basic environmental infrastructure denied to a racialized community, is called environmental racism: a term still rarely used in Portugal, but one that describes exactly the same phenomenon.
