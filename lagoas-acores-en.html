No. 60 ◐ Monitoring Freshwater + intensive farming

Eutrophication of Azores lakes by intensive dairy farming

Lagoa das Furnas and Lagoa das Sete Cidades, São Miguel, Azores

◐ Structural problem

Fertiliser and manure from dairy pastures feed algae and toxic cyanobacteria in the Furnas and Sete Cidades lakes. The Azores water management plan rates Lake Furnas and Lagoa Verde as poor and has postponed good status to 2030.

What is at stake

Both lakes fill volcanic craters on São Miguel and have been Ramsar sites since 2008. At Furnas, the Regional Government bought about 300 hectares of pasture to plant forest. At Sete Cidades, dairy farming covers a third of the basin. The Amigos dos Açores ecological association calls for public information on the cyanobacteria risk and fines for bathing in the lakes.

The lakes. The Azores water management plan rates Lake Furnas and Lagoa Verde at Sete Cidades as poor. Lake Furnas has been eutrophic for over 70 years: phosphorus and nitrogen from fertiliser and manure on dairy pastures wash in with the rain, algae and cyanobacteria grow and the water loses oxygen. The plan postponed good status to 2030.

The waterbirds. The shores and shallow waters of Lake Furnas are among the best places in the Azores for migrating waterbirds. Sete Cidades is a breeding and stopover site for ducks and waders. The Ramsar Convention lists eutrophication and the intensification of grazing as threats to these wetlands.

People who live by and swim in the lakes. Nearly all the cyanobacteria in Furnas produce toxins; since 2004 every sample contained microcystins, which attack the liver. The Amigos dos Açores ecological association warns that bathing in the lakes is a health risk and that nobody tells people. At Sete Cidades, dairy cows are the livelihood of much of the crater's population.
