Covilhã: infrastructure fund buys Portugal's largest data center and plans to expand it up to 25-fold

Covilhã, Castelo Branco district

◐ €120 million sale, deal expected to close Q1 2026

Altice Portugal sold the Covilhã data center, built by PT in 2013, to Spanish infrastructure fund Asterion Industrial Partners for €120 million. The campus currently has 6.8MW installed; the buyer is already signalling an expansion plan reaching up to 75MW on the existing site and up to 175MW on an adjacent plot with capacity already secured: a jump of up to 25-fold in capacity, with no public study yet on the water and energy consumption of that expansion.

The Covilhã data center, built by Portugal Telecom and opened in 2013, with an expansion in 2018, was sold by Altice Portugal to European infrastructure fund Asterion Industrial Partners for €120 million, through its third infrastructure fund. The deal, structured as a carve-out, is expected to close in the first quarter of 2026 and marks Asterion's entry into the Portuguese data center market.

The campus, spanning 75,500 sqm with 12,000 sqm of colocation space across 24 data halls, has capacity for more than 50,000 servers and is Tier III certified by the Uptime Institute. The cube-shaped building is surrounded by a moat fed by a rainwater collection system, creating a cooler microclimate, and the site includes its own solar photovoltaic generation and a garden with more than 600 trees.

What distinguishes this case is not new construction, but the financialization of already-built infrastructure: Asterion says it has already identified potential to expand from the current 6.8MW to 75MW within the existing campus, and up to 175MW on an adjacent plot where it has already secured power: a jump of up to 25 times the installed capacity, in an inland region where the water and energy resources available for that scale have not yet been publicly assessed. The expansion plan has, as of now, no public environmental or water-impact study.
