No. 92 ◐ Monitoring Coastal tourism development

UP12: a 1,030-bed resort threatens Albandeira's last wild coastline

Praia de Albandeira, Caramujeira, Porches, Lagoa municipality, Algarve

◐ Council reopened the ATE A1 detailed plan after it was rejected · preventive consultation closed 16 January

The civic group STOP UP12 has fought since 2023 against the UP12 tourism development, which plans 1,030 beds in hotels, aparthotels and villas on the last stretch of wild coastline between Praia da Marinha and Nossa Senhora da Rocha, in Lagoa. The detailed plan for ATE A1 was rejected by the APA, ICNF, CCDR-Algarve and DGT over serious flaws in the environmental assessment, but Lagoa Council reopened a new process to revive it in October 2025, even though the mayor himself admitted that approving this development back in 2008 "was a mistake".

The STOP UP12 Lagoa movement was formed in late 2023, among neighbours and regular visitors to the Praia de Albandeira area, in Caramujeira, Lagoa municipality, to oppose Planning Unit 12 (UP12), a tourism urbanisation plan approved in the Official Gazette on 8 February 2008, more than 16 years before the movement formed. The plan envisages hotels, apart-hotels and tourist villas totalling 1,030 beds, split across two sub-units: Sub-UOPG A (40.54 ha), between Praia da Marinha and Praia de Albandeira, divided into areas ATE A1 (5.45 ha) and ATE A2 (6.71 ha), and Sub-UOPG B (25.19 ha), in the Crastos area near Nossa Senhora da Rocha, with area ATE B (7.55 ha). The project originally included a golf course, since dropped by the developers, and was put up for sale for 89 million euros in 2018 under the commercial name "Golf Praia da Marinha". The current developer is Interfundos, a real estate company wholly owned by the bank BCP (Millennium).

In 2024, Lagoa Council submitted the detailed plans (PP) for ATE A1 and ATE A2 to public consultation. The ATE A1 plan was ultimately rejected after the Portuguese Environment Agency (APA), the Institute for Nature Conservation and Forests (ICNF), the Algarve Regional Coordination Commission (CCDR-Algarve) and the Directorate-General for Territory (DGT) issued unfavourable opinions, pointing to serious gaps in the Strategic Environmental Assessment, the absence of reasonable location alternatives, no assessment of cumulative impacts, unsustainable pressure on water resources, and harm to protected habitats and species along a karstic coastline with fragile cliffs, small beaches and immediate proximity to the Pedra do Valado Marine Protected Area.

Despite the rejection, Lagoa Municipal Council voted unanimously on 7 October 2025 to restart the elaboration of a new detailed plan for ATE A1, for a period of 18 months, published in the Official Gazette on 2 December 2025. The preventive participation period ran from 24 December 2025 to 16 January 2026, just 15 working days, coinciding with the Christmas and New Year holidays, and required prior registration and validation with the council, which STOP UP12 denounced as an obstacle to informed public participation and a breach of the spirit of the Aarhus Convention.

In 2024, the movement commissioned the environmental association A Rocha to carry out an independent ecological survey of the 220-hectare area covered by the plan, which identified two habitats protected under the EU Habitats Directive (thermo-Mediterranean and pre-desert scrub, and pseudo-steppe grassland with annuals, the latter a priority habitat for conservation), plus the Linaria algarviana flower and the Euphydryas aurinia butterfly. A Rocha recommended the area be given protected status as a "Local Area of Protected Landscape". In March 2026, Algarve chef João Marreiros, founder of the Loki restaurant in Portimão, publicly joined the movement, saying "there are places that, once destroyed, cannot be recreated".

For the movement, the central contradiction is political: according to Cecília Abecassis Empis, a STOP UP12 member, every council candidate they spoke to in the 2025 elections, including the sitting mayor Luís Encarnação, acknowledged that approving the Albandeira development in 2008 "was a mistake" and that it "will bring no benefit to the municipality", yet the council keeps reopening the process instead of seeking a solution that compensates the developers and permanently protects the land.
