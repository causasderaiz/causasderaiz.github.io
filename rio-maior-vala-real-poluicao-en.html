Maior river and Vala Real: polluting discharges every year during the tomato season

São João da Ribeira (Rio Maior), Santarém, Cartaxo and Azambuja, Santarém district, Portugal

⚡ Movements report new discharges during the tomato season (Sep 2025) and call on APA and IGAMAOT to act

The Maior river, which runs from Rio Maior to the Tagus and crosses Santarém, Cartaxo and Azambuja as the Vala Real, receives polluting discharges every year at the start of the tomato season, according to local movements who have been reporting them since 1965. In September 2025, three movements reported new discharges in São João da Ribeira, through a ditch hidden on private land, and asked the Environment Agency (APA) and the inspection authority (IGAMAOT) to stop the discharges and hold polluters accountable.

The Maior river rises in the municipality of Rio Maior and crosses the municipalities of Santarém, Cartaxo and Azambuja, where it is known as the Vala Real, before flowing into the Tagus. According to the region's environmental movements, it receives polluting discharges every year at the start of the tomato season, a situation they say has been happening since 1965 and which has been reported several times to the Portuguese Environment Agency (APA), the General Inspectorate for Agriculture, Sea, Environment and Spatial Planning (IGAMAOT) and Rio Maior Council. "The lezíria is dying", warns José Lousa, from EcoCartaxo, in a documentary by the PÓLEN collective.

In August 2023, after reports of discharges with a "red, pasty colour" and a strong smell, APA took samples at the discharge point of a tomato processing company in São João da Ribeira and in the river, upstream and downstream, to check compliance with the conditions of the company's wastewater discharge licence and the effects on the river.

On 19 September 2025, Movimento Cívico Ar Puro, EcoCartaxo and Movimento Ecologista do Vale de Santarém, members of proTEJO, reported new discharges in São João da Ribeira, coinciding with the start of the tomato season. According to the movements, the discharges were made through a stretch of ditch not visible from the public road, on private land covered by a building and vegetation, which flows into the Maior river about 800 metres downstream; with extreme drought and very low flow, the river becomes an "open sewer" every year. They asked APA and IGAMAOT to stop the discharges and hold polluters accountable, to include the Maior river in the Tagus River Basin Management Plan (2022/2027) and to reactivate the Tagus pollution monitoring commission, inactive since 2018. The only tomato processing plant in the parish declined to comment; APA and the council did not respond to Lusa.
