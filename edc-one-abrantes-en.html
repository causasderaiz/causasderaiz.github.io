EDC One: the €7 billion first Pego data center advances without assessing the impact on the Tagus

Pego Industrial Zone, former RPP Solar site, Abrantes, Santarém district

◐ Tax exemptions approved · request for more grid capacity under review

A holding company set up just over a year ago, with roughly €4,400 in share capital and no registered employees, is the promoter of an announced €7 billion investment on the former RPP Solar factory site, next to the Pego coal plant, shut down in 2021. Abrantes Council has already approved €16.2 million in tax exemptions; the Government may decide by the end of 2026 on the request for more electricity grid capacity, weighing water and environmental impact criteria. It is the first of two large-scale data centers planned for the same municipality on the Tagus.

EDC One Lda., based in Maia, emerged from the corporate rebranding of a former real-estate asset management company (PGAI), completed in August 2025. Public records show the company has share capital of roughly €4,400 and no registered employees or revenue; yet it presents itself as the promoter of a €7 billion investment in the Pego Industrial Zone in Abrantes, on the site of the former RPP Solar panel factory, next to the Pego coal-fired plant, decommissioned in 2021.

The project, named "EDC One" (a name investor José Meneses da Silva Moura says anticipates future phases: "EDC Two", "EDC Three"), is planned in three phases: 300 MW of capacity in the first, due online in the second quarter of 2028, rising to 800 MW by the end of the third phase. The promoter says it has national and international partners in the data center, construction and energy sectors, without naming them publicly.

In September 2025, Abrantes Council unanimously approved IMI, IMT and Derrama tax exemptions worth €16.2 million, under Municipal Interest Project (PIM) status, not National Interest Project (PIN) status: according to AICEP, EDC One never submitted a PIN application. The project still needs environmental licensing and final Government approvals.

In September 2026, the Environment and Energy Minister confirmed that the promoters of both data centers planned for Abrantes (EDC One/EDC Port 1 at Pego and Hyperion II near the Alvega roundabout) had requested an increase to their already-authorised grid connection capacity, and that a decision, expected before the end of 2026, would weigh energy, water and environmental impact criteria, taking into account the "special situation" of a municipality hit by the coal plant's closure. Organisations such as Quercus and press investigations warn that both projects are moving forward without a cumulative environmental impact assessment, including effects on management of the Tagus basin, where a 40% water scarcity index is projected for 2028-2033.
