No. 46 ⚡ Active situation Tungsten mining

Minerália wants to reopen tungsten mines in the Gerês-Xurés Biosphere Reserve, Montalegre, and the approval is challenged in court

Montalegre, Vila Real district

⚖ Lawsuit against the environmental approval under way, Quercus joins (Oct 2026) · European Commission lists the mine as a strategic project (9 Oct)

Minerália wants to reopen the Borralha tungsten mines, closed since 1986, inside the Gerês-Xurés Biosphere Reserve and 2 km from the Venda Nova reservoir. APA approved the project with conditions in January 2026 and, in October, the European Commission listed it as a strategic project. Local people and associations are asking the courts to annul the approval; Quercus has joined the case and the Portuguese Water Academy warns of the risk of contaminating the Cávado basin.

What is at stake

Minerália wants to reopen the Borralha mines, in Salto (Montalegre), with 15 years of underground mining of tungsten, copper and tin and a new 12-hectare waste heap. The mine lies in Barroso, a FAO Globally Important Agricultural Heritage System, inside the Gerês-Xurés Transboundary Biosphere Reserve and about 2 km from the Venda Nova reservoir, which supplies water to thousands of people in the North, including Braga.

The Venda Nova reservoir and the Cávado. The mine is 2 km from the Venda Nova reservoir, which supplies thousands of people in the North, and the Rabagão water treatment plant is just downstream. According to Quercus, the environmental decision allows surplus effluent to be released, and a discharge into the Corga do Caniçó can reach the reservoir.

What the old mine left. Studies from 2010 and 2014 found soil and water around the mine, closed in 1986, contaminated with mercury, copper, arsenic, lead and zinc. Acid drainage continues.

The oak wood and the wolf. The project means the irreversible loss of 7.48 hectares of protected oak woodland (habitat 9230) and the clearing of 20.6 hectares of forest. The region has 3 to 6 stable Iberian wolf packs, and the Amiar stream holds critically endangered fish.

People living in Salto. APA acknowledges that noise and vibration will always have a significant impact on the lives of the nearest families, for whom it proposes rehousing. Old mine galleries run beneath several villages.

  1. 19 Oct

    Quercus joins the Povo e Natureza do Barroso Association's class action seeking to have the Environmental Impact Statement declared void, arguing APA's decision "clearly violates the principles of prevention and precaution"; it warns of the risk of contaminating the Cávado basin, which supplies over a million people.

  2. 13 Oct

    The Portuguese Water Academy warns that the mine could contaminate the Cávado basin, which supplies about 400,000 people in Braga and Barcelos, and calls for an epidemiological study carried out from scratch, with "absolute laboratory independence".

    Source: Observador/Lusa ↗
  3. 9 Oct

    The European Commission selects the Minerália project as one of the EU's 46 new strategic critical raw materials projects, for tungsten extraction. Movimento Não às Minas Montalegre notes that the classification "does not in itself constitute an environmental or social validation of the project".

    Source: BragaTV ↗
  4. 7 Jun

    The Movimento Não às Minas contests the monitoring commission set up by Minerália, arguing that this is a State competence.

    Source: Diário de Trás-os-Montes ↗
  5. Apr

    The Povo e Natureza do Barroso Association files a lawsuit against the Environmental Impact Statement, on behalf of the Movimento Não às Minas Montalegre, the commons of Caniçó, Linharelhos and Campos and the Iris and APIFC associations.

    Source: O Minho ↗
  6. 8 Jan

    The Environment Agency issues a conditionally favourable opinion on the project, with conditions including the rehousing of residents affected by noise and vibration.

    Source: RTP ↗
