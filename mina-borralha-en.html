Borralha Mine: reactivated tungsten mining threatens Cávado water quality

Montalegre, Vila Real district

⚡ Petition demands unfavourable EIS · Braga City Council warns of risk to Cávado (Sept 9) · Legal action ongoing

A petition by the Cávado Sem Minas collective calls on basin councils to reject the project, after Braga City Council warned of a risk to Cávado drinking water; six associations had already taken a tungsten mine's Environmental Impact Statement to court, in the Gerês-Xurés Biosphere Reserve.

On 13 September 2026, the citizens' collective Cávado Sem Minas launched a public petition addressed to the municipal assemblies and executives of the nine municipalities in the Cávado basin (Amares, Barcelos, Braga, Esposende, Montalegre, Póvoa de Lanhoso, Terras de Bouro, Vieira do Minho and Vila Verde), to CIM Cávado, CIM Ave, the Portuguese Environment Agency and the Ministry of Environment. The petition states that, during the public consultation, Agere and Águas do Norte warned of risks of heavy-metal contamination from the Venda Nova and Alto Rabagão reservoirs to drinking-water intakes, and that EDP raised objections about the safety of the Venda Nova dam in the event of blasting and extreme weather. It also notes that, of the nine municipalities in the basin, only Montalegre responded to the public consultation, and calls on the municipal assemblies to pass motions demanding an unfavourable Environmental Impact Statement for the project.

In September 2026, the concern spread to Braga: Agere, the municipal water, wastewater and waste company, told the Portuguese Environment Agency it had serious concerns about the impact a reactivation of the mine could have on the quality of water drawn from the Cávado river, which supplies the city. Braga's mayor, João Rodrigues, said the municipality is alert and attentive to the process, and councillor Filipe Aguiar stressed that the water Braga residents drink daily comes from the Cávado river, and that its quality could be affected, not only in Braga but in the other municipalities the river passes through.

In May 2026, Minerália announced the creation of a Monitoring Committee and Social Observatory for the project, bringing together Montalegre Council, Salto Parish Council, the University of Minho's School of Sciences, commons associations, firefighters, local associations, residents and farmers from the area. The company presents it as the first initiative of its kind linked to mining activity in Portugal, aimed at permanently monitoring the mine's development.

The association Povo e Natureza do Barroso has filed a legal action against the Environmental Impact Statement for the Borralha mine project in Montalegre, Vila Real district. The action also represents the Movimento Não às Minas – Montalegre, the governing councils of the Caniçó, Linharelhos and Campos commons, Iris (National Environment Association) and APIFC. The project, to mine tungsten through underground galleries inactive since 1986, received a conditional favourable opinion from the Portuguese Environment Agency in January 2026, with the concession held by the company Minerália; Montalegre's municipal council had already issued a negative opinion.

The area lies within the Gerês-Xurés Transboundary Biosphere Reserve, six kilometres from Peneda-Gerês National Park, and is part of the FAO's Globally Important Agricultural Heritage System, in one of northern Portugal's most important water resource areas. The associations warn of the risk to the Rabagão water treatment plant, located downstream of the project, and of the unresolved environmental liability from the old Borralha mine, still pending four decades after its closure.

The public consultation on the environmental impact study, held between October and November 2025, received 653 submissions, many concerning drinking water intake at the Venda Nova dam downstream of the mine. The associations also report unanswered complaints about pollution in the Amiar stream. Minerália says it will use 100% recycled water, with no abstraction from rivers, dams or aquifers.
