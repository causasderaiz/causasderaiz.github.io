No. 46 ⚡ Active situation Tungsten mining

Borralha Mine (Salto, Montalegre)

Montalegre, Vila Real district

⚖ Legal action against the APA ongoing · Project approved with conditions in January 2026

Minerália wants to reopen the Borralha tungsten mines, closed since 1986, inside the Gerês-Xurés Biosphere Reserve and 2 km from the Venda Nova reservoir; local communities and associations are challenging the project in court.

The territory

The Borralha Mines, in the parish of Salto, were one of the country's main tungsten centres between 1902 and 1986, employing around 2000 people at their peak. They closed with no alternatives or compensation for those who worked there. They left behind large spoil heaps, and soils and water contaminated with mercury, copper, arsenic, lead and chromium.

The mine lies in Barroso, a territory classified by the FAO as a Globally Important Agricultural Heritage System. It is inside the Gerês-Xurés Transboundary Biosphere Reserve, about 6 km from Peneda-Gerês National Park and 2 km from the Venda Nova reservoir, which supplies water to thousands of people in the North, including Braga.

The project

The concession was awarded to the company Minerália in 2021. The project plans 15 years of underground tungsten mining, with copper and tin, and a new 12-hectare spoil heap. The public consultation received 653 submissions. In January 2026, the Portuguese Environment Agency (APA) approved the project with conditions, including the rehousing of residents.

The conflict

Local people fear water contamination, a new mining liability in a territory that was never cleaned up, the collapse of old galleries beneath the villages, the lack of any assessment of radioactive risk, and the impacts on the Iberian wolf and on farming and pastoral life.

In April 2026, the Movimento Não às Minas Montalegre and the Povo e Natureza do Barroso Association filed a class action (ação popular) against the APA to stop the project, supported by several commons councils and by the associations Iris and APIFC.

Who is resisting

Resistance to the Borralha Mine comes from the directly affected local communities, organised since 2019. This struggle has already helped stop several mining projects in the region. The communities are represented by the municipality's collectives:

Movimento Não às Minas Montalegre, which has led the opposition to the Borralha and Romano mines since 2019.

Povo e Natureza do Barroso Association, the Movimento's partner in the class action against the APA.

Montalegre Com Vida Association, dedicated to the fight against the Romano Mine.
