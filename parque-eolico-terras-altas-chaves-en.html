Terras Altas de Chaves Wind Farm: 20 turbines on the common lands where wolves breed

Faiões, Sobreira, São Julião de Montenegro and Avelelas, Chaves (power line to Valpaços)

🗳 Public consultation until 25 November 2026 · Scoping of the environmental impact study (APA)

🗳 Take part in the public consultation

AER SOLÉIR wants to install 20 wind turbines, 140 MW, on 668 hectares of four common lands in the Chaves uplands, with a power line to Valpaços. The developer's own document confirms a pair of Iberian wolves there with two cubs born in 2026. Until 25 November, what the environmental impact study must assess is open to public consultation.

What is at stake

In the Chaves uplands, between Faiões, Sobreira, São Julião de Montenegro and Avelelas, AER SOLÉIR Investments Portugal wants to build the Terras Altas de Chaves Wind Farm: 20 turbines of 7 MW, 140 MW in total, on 668 hectares of four adjoining common lands (baldios), leased for 40 years. The power would run along an overhead line to the Valpaços substation, with two routes under study. The area lies within the Chaves Forest Perimeter and crosses the Tâmega ecological corridor. The developer's own document records a pair of Iberian wolves and two cubs born in 2026. The scoping of the environmental impact study, the stage that decides what the study must assess, is under public consultation.

The wolf pack. Since May 2026, ten camera traps have recorded at least one adult pair of Iberian wolves using the wind farm area and, at the end of August, two cubs: the pack breeds there. The data match the Nogueira da Montanha pack, confirmed in the 2019/2021 national wolf census. The developer acknowledges an exclusion effect on the wolf, worse during the breeding season.

The birds of prey. The document lists ten birds threatened under the Red List of Birds of mainland Portugal (2022) that may use the area, including Montagu's harrier, goshawk, peregrine falcon, hobby, scops owl and long-eared owl.

The common lands and woods. The 668 hectares are village common lands (baldios), managed by the commoners, within the Chaves Forest Perimeter. The area has Pyrenean oak woods, old chestnut groves, riverside alder woods and grasslands that are priority habitats in Europe, and threatened flora such as the orchid Orchis provincialis, Critically Endangered. Platforms, access roads and cable trenches alter the relief, soil and natural drainage.
