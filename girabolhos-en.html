No. 08 ⚡ Active petition Dam

A Girabolhos dam could flood the Mondego valley, and GEOTA, ZERO and LPN are contesting it

Girabolhos, Seia; reservoir across Seia, Gouveia, Fornos de Algodres, Mangualde and Nelas, Mondego river

⚡ Public tender for the concession open since 15 July 2026 (270 days for bids)

⚡ Sign the petition

On 15 July 2026 the Government launched the tender for a 65-year concession for the Girabolhos dam on the upper Mondego, with at least 40 MW and water up to 300 metres above sea level. The project, awarded to Endesa in 2008 and revoked in 2016, returns without an up-to-date environmental impact assessment: the 2010 statement expired in 2014. GEOTA, ZERO, LPN and ClimaAção Centro contest the dam and its flood-control justification.

What is at stake

The Government presents the dam as a response to the Lower Mondego floods, to water supply and to electricity generation, and the Minister of Environment and Energy, Maria da Graça Carvalho, called it "essential and strategic". The associations contest that justification. ZERO calculates that the catchment draining to Girabolhos covers 980 km², less than 15% of the Mondego basin, and notes that the tender documents set no flood-attenuation rules. ClimaAção Centro points out that the dam lies about 70 km from the Lower Mondego, with the Aguieira dam in between. GEOTA attributes the floods to deforestation, silting, building on floodplains and the loss of wetlands. The winning bidder will have 60 months to submit the environmental impact study, and the head of APA expects the works to be completed by 2038. ZERO and GEOTA call for a new assessment comparing alternatives, such as reforesting the mountains upstream and nature-based solutions.

The Iberian desman. The stretch of the Mondego where the dam would be built is an Important Site for the Conservation of the Iberian Desman (no. 26, Mondego River), and the Torto river and the Linhares stream, tributaries reached by the reservoir, are places of high importance for the species, according to APA's 2014 opinion. The Iberian desman is classified as Endangered in the Red Book of the Mammals of Mainland Portugal (2023); in 2005 it was Vulnerable. APA required corridors between the streams flowing into the reservoir, linked to the populations of the Serra da Estrela Natura 2000 site.

Fish and river wildlife. The brown trout, Near Threatened in the Red Book of Freshwater and Diadromous Fish (2023), lives in this stretch and needs fast-flowing, well-oxygenated water. APA criticised the 2011 design for setting the ecological flow on the basis of the Iberian nase, the Iberian barbel and the calandino, less demanding species. The dam wall and reservoir cut the river off for fish, otters, Schreiber's green lizard and freshwater mussels, and APA asked for passage devices.

The Mondego daffodil and the bats. Surveys in 2011 found four subpopulations of the Mondego daffodil (Narcissus scaberulus) in the area to be flooded by the Bogueira reservoir, downstream of Girabolhos. The reservoirs also flood roosts of cave-dwelling bats, which APA ordered to be replaced with built shelters.

The valley. The reservoir would submerge one of the last near-natural stretches of the Mondego, with riverside woodland that GEOTA counts among the best on the river, farmland and old paths: APA's opinion lists historic paved tracks and bridges that would end up under water. Most of the flooded land lies in the municipality of Gouveia.

People downstream. The floods used to justify the dam happen in the Lower Mondego, about 70 km away, in a basin that the dam does not control for more than 85% of its area, according to ZERO. The associations call for a public study of the floods and for measures across the basin itself: reforesting the Açor and Lousã mountains, restoring rivers and fixing the hydraulic system.
