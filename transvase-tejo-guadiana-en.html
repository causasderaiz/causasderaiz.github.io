Tagus–Guadiana water transfer: CCDR Alentejo studies inter-basin link

Guadiana basin, Alentejo (link under study with the Tagus river)

◐ CCDR Alentejo and EDIA move forward with EU funding bid for study (8 Sep) · ZERO warns of risks

The Alentejo Regional Coordination and Development Commission (CCDR) and EDIA met on 8 September to prepare a bid for EU funds to finance an in-depth study on the feasibility of transferring water from the Tagus river to the Guadiana basin, under the national “Água que Une” ("Water that Unites") strategy. The scenario under consideration envisages diverting around 150 cubic hectometres of water, which would allow Alqueva-supported irrigation to expand by more than 50,000 new hectares. The environmental association ZERO warns that continuously expanding irrigated area could exhaust the safety reserve the Alqueva reservoir is meant to guarantee in the long run, and that inter-basin transfers tend to generate territorial tensions between donor and recipient regions. The Environment Minister has already downplayed the Tagus–Guadiana link as not "the first priority", noting that such a project would require an agreement with Spain. For now there is no green light for construction: the process is solely aimed at securing funding for a technical and economic assessment.

The Alentejo Regional Coordination and Development Commission (CCDR) and EDIA met on 8 September 2026 to prepare a bid for the Alentejo 2030 Regional Programme to fund a study on the feasibility of transferring water from the Tagus river to the Guadiana basin, under the national "Água que Une" ("Water that Unites") strategy. According to CCDR Alentejo's president, Ricardo Pinheiro, the scenario under study envisages diverting around 150 cubic hectometres of water, which would allow Alqueva-supported irrigation to expand by more than 50,000 new hectares. The study, not yet funded, is meant to assess both the engineering side of the link and the economic impact of the investment across different sub-regions of the Alentejo.

The environmental association ZERO warns that continuously expanding irrigated area could exhaust the safety reserve the Alqueva reservoir is meant to guarantee in the long run, and notes that inter-basin transfers tend to generate territorial tensions between donor and recipient regions.

The Minister of Environment and Energy, Maria da Graça Carvalho, has for now downplayed the Tagus-Guadiana link as a priority, arguing that "this kind of inter-basin link should always be the last solution, after every other solution has been tried". According to the minister, the Alentejo's water reserves currently stand at 80% (the national average), and other responses are already under way, including Alqueva, existing dams and a planned desalination plant in Sines; any link would also require agreement between the Portuguese and Spanish governments, since the Tagus and Guadiana are international rivers.

For now there is no green light for construction: the process is solely aimed at securing funding for a technical and economic assessment.
