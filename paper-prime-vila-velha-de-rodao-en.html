No. 172 ◐ Monitoring Paper industry

Paper Prime seeks a licence to go from 87.7 to 130 tonnes a day and its own water intake on the Tagus, by the Portas de Ródão, in Vila Velha de Ródão

Vila Velha de Ródão Industrial Zone, Castelo Branco district

🗳 Public consultation until 15 December 2026 · APA case no. PL20260526004303 (EIA and IPPC)

🗳 Take part in the public consultation

Paper Prime, a tissue paper mill in Vila Velha de Ródão, is seeking a licence to go from 87.7 to 130 tonnes a day, 48% more, and says the machine's capacity was incorrectly declared at licensing. It also wants its own water intake on the Tagus, inside the Portas de Ródão Natural Monument. Discharges go into the Fratel reservoir, where proTEJO denounces pollution. Public consultation until 15 December.

What is at stake

Paper Prime, part of the Socigene group (Joker brand), has made tissue paper reels in the Vila Velha de Ródão industrial zone since 2016, next to Biotek (formerly Celtejo), which supplies it with pulp by pipeline. It is licensed for 87.7 tonnes a day. The environmental impact study says the capacity actually installed on the paper machine was incorrectly declared when the mill was licensed, and now asks for 130 tonnes a day, 48% more. Some of the changes are already done: a new press on the paper machine, solar panels, improvements to the treatment plant and an electric boiler. Still to be built are its own water intake on the Tagus, a 3 km pipeline and a water treatment unit. Treated wastewater goes through Biotek's outfall into the Fratel reservoir, on the stretch where proTEJO has denounced foam and dark water for more than a decade.

The Tagus and the Fratel reservoir. The mill's treated wastewater goes through Biotek's outfall into the Fratel reservoir. At the requested capacity, discharges rise from about 163,000 to 201,000 cubic metres a year and river water use from 235,000 to 289,000. This is the stretch where proTEJO has denounced foam and dark water since at least 2015.

The Portas de Ródão. The new intake, a floating raft on the Tagus, and the first 150 metres of the pipeline lie inside the Portas de Ródão Natural Monument, the quartzite gorge the Tagus cuts through, in the Naturtejo Geopark.

Those who live in Ródão. The study itself acknowledges that the treatment plant's homogenisation tank produced hydrogen sulphide and that the sludge released odorous compounds, problems it says were fixed in 2024. The mill runs 24 hours a day, next to Biotek and Navigator.
