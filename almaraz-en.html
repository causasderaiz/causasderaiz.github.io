Almaraz: associations go to court against extending the Spanish nuclear plant

Almaraz, Cáceres, Spain (Tagus basin, 100 km from the Portuguese border)

⚖ Legal action announced

The Spanish government extended the Almaraz nuclear plant's operation until 2030, in the Tagus basin. Portuguese and Spanish associations are taking the decision to court.

Spain's Ministry for Ecological Transition renewed the operating licence for Units I and II of the Almaraz Nuclear Power Plant, in Cáceres, in the Tagus river basin, about 100 kilometres from the Portuguese border, until June 2030. The decision follows a formal request for the extension made in October 2025 by the plant's three owners, Iberdrola, Endesa and Naturgy.

The gradual shutdown of the two reactors, operating since the early 1980s, had been scheduled for November 2027 (Unit I) and October 2028 (Unit II), under a protocol already agreed between the companies and the Spanish state. The movement "Alargar as Nucleares, Não Obrigado" ("Extend Nuclear Plants, No Thanks"), which brings together Adenex, Ecologistas en Acción and Greenpeace, calls the extension "illegal" for breaking that agreement.

The Iberian Energy Observatory, which brings together Portuguese and Spanish associations, announced it will go to court "as soon as possible" against the decision, with the case to be filed in Spain by Adenex. The associations warn that extending Almaraz could also jeopardise the shutdown schedule for Spain's other nuclear plants.
