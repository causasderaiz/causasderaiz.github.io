Matosinhos: polluting discharges and foul smells threaten the Leça river and bathing beaches

Leça river and streams of Leça da Palmeira and Matosinhos

⚡ Matosinhos beach at risk of losing its official bathing-water status · Councillors demand inspections

Polluting discharges from the area of Galp's former refinery in Leça da Palmeira left a "nauseating smell" affecting the city in April; the Portuguese Environment Agency is now pursuing a fine against Galp. The problem runs deeper: around 900 illegal connections dump wastewater directly into the Riguinha and Carcavelos streams and the Leça river, and episodes of microbiological contamination have already led to temporary bathing bans at Matosinhos beach, which risks losing its official "Bathing Water" status. In August, PSD and Chega councillors demanded the council adopt an integrated plan to restore the streams, inspect illegal connections, and monitor water quality publicly in real time.

Discharges of polluting waste from the site of Galp's former refinery in Leça da Palmeira left a "nauseating smell" affecting Matosinhos in April 2026. Galp confirmed the occurrence at a decommissioned part of the old refinery, saying it was "under mitigation" and posed no risk to public health; the Portuguese Environment Agency is now processing a fine against the company.

The problem runs deeper: Matosinhos City Council has detected around 900 illegal connections discharging wastewater directly into the Riguinha and Carcavelos streams, which flow out near Matosinhos beach. Episodes of microbiological contamination have already led to temporary bathing bans (the beach was closed 18 times in the last bathing season) and it risks losing its official "Bathing Water" status.

In August 2026, PSD and Chega councillors demanded the council draw up an integrated stream-recovery plan, inspect illegal connections and set up public real-time water-quality monitoring. The municipality has already corrected around 200 of the illegal connections found.
