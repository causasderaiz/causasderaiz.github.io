MUDA: against tree felling and poor management of urban tree cover

Greater Lisbon, with nationwide action

⚡ Active petition · 1,723 signatures · survey of councils breaching Law 59/2021

MUDA is a citizens' collective, independent of political parties and authorities, that campaigns for the protection of trees and urban tree cover on climate, biodiversity and quality of life grounds. It launched a nationwide petition against the mass felling of trees by Infraestruturas de Portugal along roads and railways, including inside Sintra-Cascais Natural Park, often in breach of Law 59/2021, which sets the legal framework for urban tree management. Beyond the petition, it met with Sintra Council, contacted parliamentary groups, carried out direct action on the ground and surveyed councils breaching the law, identifying specific cases in Cabeço de Vide, Amadora, Sintra, Lisbon and Silves.

MUDA (Movimento Muda de Árvore, also known as the Movement for the Union in Defence of Trees) denounces the systematic felling of trees along roadsides and railways across the country, carried out by Infraestruturas de Portugal (IP) and by local municipalities, often without prior notice or environmental impact assessment.

In March 2024, the movement joined 17 other environmental associations in a public petition that described the felling as an “ecocide” and called for intervention by the Government, municipal councils, ICNF and APA. In Rio Maior, a group of citizens even took a case over tree felling on the EN 114 road to court.

In March 2025, MUDA held a protest on Avenida 5 de Outubro in Lisbon, hanging banners against the felling of jacaranda trees to make way for an underground car park, a case that gathered over 54,000 signatures and became the most-supported citizen petition in the city's history. Spokesperson Rui Mourão pointed to “serious procedural irregularities” by Lisbon City Council, namely regarding Law 59/2021, the municipal tree management regulation and ICNF's code of good practice.

MUDA is calling for compliance with Law 59/2021, which regulates tree felling in public space, is compiling a map of municipalities in breach of it, and has asked Parliament to finally regulate the penalties set out in the law, which the Government has still not defined more than four years on.
