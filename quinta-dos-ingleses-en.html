Quinta dos Ingleses: court rules on bid to halt 850-apartment development in the Carcavelos pine forest

Carcavelos, Cascais, Lisbon district

⚖ Works suspended by injunction · court hearings under way in September 2026

Cascais Municipal Council approved a detailed plan enabling around 850 apartments, a hotel and a shopping centre on a centuries-old, roughly 52-hectare pine forest next to Carcavelos beach. Since October 2025, a popular legal action by the 66 Pela Quinta movement has kept the works suspended under an injunction; the Sintra Administrative and Fiscal Court is holding trial hearings in September 2026.

Quinta dos Ingleses, also known as Quinta Nova de Santo António, is a centuries-old pine forest (planted between 1900 and 1920) covering around 52 hectares next to Carcavelos beach, in Cascais municipality. In 2021, the Portuguese Parliament passed a resolution recommending that Cascais Council and the Government classify Quinta dos Ingleses as a locally protected landscape; the council, by political choice, never followed through on that classification.

Cascais Council approved the Detailed Plan for the Carcavelos South Urban Restructuring Area (PPERUCS), covering around 54 hectares (including the St. Julian's School site), enabling construction of around 850 apartments, a hotel and a shopping centre, with an approximately eight-hectare urban park planned in return. In December 2024, an injunction filed by the SOS Quinta dos Ingleses association was dismissed by a judge who had previously worked as a legal adviser at Cascais Council with direct contact with the case file; works resumed "at full speed" in April 2025, with trucks leaving the site loaded with felled trees.

In October 2025, the civic movement 66 Pela Quinta filed a new popular legal action against Cascais Municipality, naming construction company Alves Ribeiro and St. Julian's School as interested parties, seeking to suspend the lot-subdivision decision; the resulting injunction halted the works. Trial hearings, postponed several times, began in September 2026 at the Sintra Administrative and Fiscal Court, with sessions scheduled for the 9th, 11th, 25th and 28th of September. On 11 September, a Cascais Council legal officer argued in court that the detailed plan was highly thorough, that the approved subdivision fell short of the plan's maximum parameters (about 10,000 m² less building footprint, about 40,000 m² more permeable area, and 80 fewer housing units than allowed), and that the environmental impact assessment had concluded positive impacts outweighed negative ones. The 66 Pela Quinta movement argues instead for the area to be classified as a natural park, and warns that PPERUCS's environmental impact study is over a decade old, outdated relative to ongoing climate change and coastal-retreat risk.

Context note: in a separate but related process, a criminal complaint filed in September 2023 by SOS Quinta dos Ingleses, prompted by the council's 2020 sale, for €312,000, of a roughly 830 m² municipal plot on which the "Hilton Cascais Residences" development now stands on the Carcavelos seafront road, led to a Judicial Police investigation into Cascais Council. Following an RTP investigative report in July 2026, the Public Prosecutor's Office filed an action at the Sintra Administrative and Fiscal Court against the Municipality, the landowner (Encosta da Parede) and mortgage creditors, seeking to void the entire licensing process and have what has been built demolished, or at minimum three of the seven floors of the two residential towers already constructed, for exceeding the building ratio allowed for the zone. Cascais Council announced it would contest the Public Prosecutor's decision.
