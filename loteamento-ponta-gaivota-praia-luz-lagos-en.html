Residents oppose a 22-villa development on the Ponta da Gaivota cliffs at Praia da Luz

Mata Porcas (Ponta da Gaivota), between Porto Dona Maria and Reserva da Luz, Luz parish, Lagos municipality, Faro district

⚡ Active · Amendment to allotment permit 1/74 (case no. 6/2021) at Lagos Council, no known final decision

In 2021, Celestino Vermelho Rodrigues asked Lagos Council to amend an allotment approved in 1974 at Mata Porcas, on Ponta da Gaivota, between Porto Dona Maria and Reserva da Luz: 22 two-storey villas on a 14.6-hectare plot, with the seaward lots inside the National Ecological Reserve (REN), which protects the cliffs and where the law forbids subdividing and building. The Miradouro da Luz association points out that the council itself declared the permit expired in 1985 and 1986; in September 2022 the council halted the clearing of the land, and in January 2023 the association handed in a 613-signature petition and complained to IGAMAOT, the environmental inspectorate. The council holds that the permit is still valid, the Algarve regional authority CCDR, which oversees the REN, accepted it on that basis in October 2022 after two unfavourable opinions, and no final decision on the amendment is known.

What is at stake

Lagos mayor Hugo Pereira told DN that «the municipality has not identified any illegality that could lead to the immediate rejection of the application» and that the amendment does not touch the REN. In a written reply to Público, the council argued that expiry required an express declaration and notice to the permit holder, documents not found in the file. The Miradouro da Luz association says the council declared the allotment expired in 1985 and 1986, with only about 10% of the infrastructure built; that the Lagos master plan classifies almost all of the land as ecological protection space; that the project should undergo environmental impact assessment because it exceeds 10 hectares; and that the permit was only entered in the land registry in 2020. The association says it will go to court if necessary. According to Público, CCDR Algarve was asked to cooperate with the IGAMAOT investigation. The land is not in Natura 2000 or a protected area, according to the CCDR.

The cliffs and their protection strip. The Lagos REN map places part of the land in the cliff protection strip, where the law forbids subdividing, opening roads, building, digging and removing vegetation, according to CCDR Algarve itself. Lots 23 to 29, on the seaward side, lie inside that strip, as does the southern access road.

The open ground between Luz and Porto Dona Maria. The 14.6-hectare plot is open ground between the Reserva da Luz development and Porto Dona Maria, beside the clifftop path from Luz to Burgau. According to the Miradouro da Luz association, the master plan classifies almost all of it as ecological protection space; in 2022 clearing in an unlicensed area led the council to halt the works.

A permit from 1974. The 22 villas rest on a permit more than 50 years old that Lagos Council declared expired in 1985 and 1986, according to the association and Público, and that the same council now considers valid. For the association, a project of this size should undergo environmental impact assessment and today's rules, not a 1974 plan.
