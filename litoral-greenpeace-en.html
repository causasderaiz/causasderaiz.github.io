No. 13 ⚡ Active petition Coastline and climate change

Greenpeace warns a quarter of Portugal's coast is retreating and calls for a building moratorium

Continental coastline, especially Sesimbra and Grândola

⚡ Report and petition active

Greenpeace's "Destruição a Toda a Costa" report finds that 25% of Portugal's continental coastline is eroding and more than 100,000 people live in high-risk coastal areas. The organisation points to Sesimbra and Grândola as examples of outdated municipal plans licensing luxury tourism megaprojects in protected areas, and calls for a moratorium on construction in flood- or erosion-risk areas through 2050.

The report "Destruição a Toda a Costa: Retrato de um litoral português em risco" ("Destruction All Along the Coast"), published by Greenpeace Portugal on 23 July 2026, finds that 25% of mainland Portugal's coastline is eroding and that more than 100,000 people live in at-risk coastal areas, as sea level rises 3.7 mm a year, faster than the global average.

The organisation singles out the municipalities of Sesimbra and Grândola, in Setúbal district, as examples of outdated municipal master plans that keep licensing large tourism developments in protected areas: in Sesimbra, projects such as Pinhal da Prata, Pinhal do Atlântico and Étosoto occupy more than 260 hectares of protected land, moving forward without adequate environmental assessment.

Greenpeace estimates that by 2100 up to 50% of Alentejo's beaches and around 40% of the Algarve's beaches could disappear, and is calling on the government for a moratorium on construction in flood- or erosion-risk areas until 2050.
