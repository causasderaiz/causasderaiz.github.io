Montagu's harrier: combine harvesters crush the nests of an endangered bird

Cereal fields of the Alentejo and Trás-os-Montes

◐ Endangered · population down 80% in ten years · LIFE SOS Pygargus campaign in the field

Montagu's harrier nests on the ground, in the middle of cereal fields. With cereal replaced by fodder crops and earlier harvests, mowing catches nests with eggs and chicks. The species is Endangered in Portugal and its population has fallen 80% in ten years. The LIFE SOS Pygargus project, led by Palombar, asks farmers to warn before harvesting and leave an uncut strip around nests.

What is at stake

Pairs breeding in Portugal are about 13% of the European population (excluding Russia). The loss of grain cereal, conversion to permanent crops, the switch to fodder and climate change have pushed harvesting into the nesting season. In 2026 the Alentejo agriculture directorate set minimum harvest dates and asks for five working days' notice so nests can be searched for. LIFE SOS Pygargus brings together Palombar, SPEA, LPN, ICNF, EDIA, INIAV and other partners in Portugal and Spain.

The bird. Montagu's harrier is Endangered in the Portuguese Red Book of Vertebrates. Its population has fallen 80% in ten years, with sharp drops in areas such as Campo Maior.

The nests. Machines cutting cereal and fodder destroy eggs and chicks without noticing them. It is one of the main threats to the species' breeding.

The fields. A single pair eats more than a thousand crop-damaging animals during the breeding season. Protecting a nest means leaving at least 10 metres uncut around it.
