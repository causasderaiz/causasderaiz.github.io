No. 185 ◐ Monitoring Extra-high-voltage line

REN's extra-high-voltage lines run close to villages between Ferreira do Alentejo, Ourique and Tavira

Ferreira do Alentejo, Aljustrel, Castro Verde, Ourique, Almodôvar, Loulé (Ameixial) and Tavira (Cachopo), Beja and Faro districts

⚡ Lines in service since December 2024 · EIA procedure no. 3438 (APA)

REN, the company that runs Portugal's extra-high-voltage grid, built about 150 km of new lines with more than 400 pylons between 2022 and 2024, from Ferreira do Alentejo to Tavira, plus a new substation at Panóias, Ourique, to carry power from solar plants in the Baixo Alentejo and the Algarve. During the environmental assessment, Loulé council asked for the route to move away from the villages of Vale da Moita and Corte João Marques, in Ameixial, and the nature conservation group LPN criticised the study for ignoring the solar plants the line is meant to serve. The lines have been in service since December 2024, and the Government authorised felling 149 cork oaks and 45 holm oaks.

What is at stake

The axis is the connection the solar plants planned around Panóias depend on, including Bartolomeu Dias and Ourique II (conflict 175), and the Mira wind farm, whose 150 kV line reaches the Ourique substation (conflict 36). In the public consultation, from 26 November 2021 to 10 January 2022, the APA received three submissions: from the Directorate-General for Territory, from LPN and from a citizen who considered the works unjustified.

People living by the pylons. The environmental impact statement records pylons next to Vale da Moita and Corte João Marques, in Ameixial, and Alcarias Pedro Guerreiro, in Cachopo, with permanent and significant impacts. Loulé council asked for the route to move away from these villages. According to local residents, the pylons and lines stand close to homes, bring noise and take value from properties and quiet from those who live there. In Garvão and Funcheira, in Ourique, according to a Funcheira homeowner, the lines run about 120 metres from the houses, and the planned route of the Mira wind farm line is also expected to pass nearby.

Birds of the plains and the hills. The great bustard, the little bustard and the black stork, all with unfavourable conservation status, live in the area of the lines, and the APA counts another 64 species at risk of colliding with the cables. Some pylons stand in dry cereal fields, habitat of steppe birds, and the Panóias–Tavira line crosses the Ribeira do Vascão Ramsar site.

The montado. According to the statement, around 116 pylons stand in cork and holm oak woodland, a habitat of high conservation value. Order no. 989/2025 authorised felling 149 cork oaks and 45 holm oaks across 8.27 hectares, from Ferreira do Alentejo to Cachopo.
