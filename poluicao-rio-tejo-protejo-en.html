No. 104 ⚡ Active situation River pollution

proTEJO reports new resurgence of pollution in the Tagus river

From the Upper Tagus (Vila Velha de Ródão) to Chamusca, in the Tagus Lezíria

⚡ proTEJO demands urgent testing and accountability for polluters (Jun 2026)

The proTEJO movement reported, in June 2026, a resurgence of pollution in the Tagus river between Vila Velha de Ródão and Chamusca, with abundant foam and darkened waters, a pattern the movement has documented for over a decade and attributes to industrial and urban effluent discharges along the Upper Tagus. proTEJO is demanding that the Portuguese Environment Agency and IGAMAOT carry out full water tests, conduct night-time inspections of discharges, and suspend effluent discharge licences in cases of non-compliance.

proTEJO, the Movement for the Tagus, reported on 2 June 2026 a resurgence of pollution in the Tagus river, which it attributes to effluent discharges from Vila Velha de Ródão, in the Upper Tagus, down to Chamusca, in the Tagus Lezíria. In a statement, the movement reported episodes of abundant foam and dark waters, now yellowish in tone, recorded near the Belver dam and at points such as Ortiga (Mação), Abrantes, Constância and Vila Nova da Barquinha.

According to proTEJO, the pattern is the same one the movement has documented and denounced for over a decade: already in January 2026 it had reported to IGAMAOT abundant foam between the Fratel dam and Barca da Amieira, and in May new images near the Belver dam again confirmed the situation. The movement notes that, since at least 2015, the Tagus has been cyclically affected by intense pollution episodes originating in the Upper Tagus, and that in 2018 the Portuguese Environment Agency itself confirmed cellulose fibre levels thousands of times above recommended values, linked to discharges from the Celtejo pulp mill in Vila Velha de Ródão.

proTEJO is demanding immediate full water-quality testing upstream and downstream of the Vila Velha de Ródão river pier, regular inspection of discharges including at night, public disclosure of monitoring-station data, the review or suspension of effluent discharge licences in cases of proven non-compliance, and the urgent scheduling of a meeting of the Tagus River Pollution Monitoring Committee with the Ministry of Environment and Energy, APA, IGAMAOT and civil society representatives. The movement also criticises the failure to deliver on a promise made in 2019 to modernise the National Water Resources Information System for real-time data, similar to Spain's SAIH system.
