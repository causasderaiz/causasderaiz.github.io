Alviela river: half a century of polluting discharges between Alcanena and Pernes

Alviela river, from Alcanena to Pernes (Santarém), Santarém district, Portugal

⚡ New discharge in Pernes in February 2026; nine organisations demand structural solutions from the Government

The Alviela river, a tributary of the Tagus, still receives polluting discharges, with foam and foul smells in Pernes (Santarém), especially after heavy rain. Organisations point to the lack of capacity of the Alcanena wastewater treatment plant and to livestock farms upstream; nine associations are demanding structural solutions from the Government, and CLAPA, founded in 1976 to fight the pollution of the Alviela, became active again in 2026.

The Alviela river, a tributary of the Tagus in the Santarém district, has suffered polluting discharges for decades. In 1976 the Commission for the Fight against the Pollution of the Alviela (CLAPA), one of the first environmental associations in the country, was founded in Pernes; its founder, Joaquim Jorge Duarte, had already taken bottles of river water to Parliament in 1970 to show the pollution.

In December 2025, a new pollution episode in Pernes, with "abundant foam and a foul smell", according to residents and CLAPA, led nine organisations (CLAPA, GEOTA, proTEJO, WWF Portugal, FAPAS, ZERO, SPEA, LPN and Quercus) to denounce the lack of structural responses. According to the organisations, the Alcanena wastewater treatment plant, upstream, lacks the capacity to cope after heavy rain, and wastewater reaches the river untreated. They warned that these discharges are incompatible with the ecological restoration of the river under the Rios Livres programme, led by GEOTA since 2022, and asked the Government for political commitment, identified structural solutions, public monitoring of water quality and participation of communities and associations in decisions. CLAPA's president, Penélope de Melo, said it is unacceptable that, "after half a century of struggle", the Alviela continues to suffer unpunished environmental attacks.

On 11 February 2026, on a rainy day, the river again showed brown foam and a foul smell in Pernes, which CLAPA attributed to effluent from livestock farms upstream of the village. The association says it has repeatedly reported the problem without any response from the authorities; residents complained that the GNR's environmental unit (SEPNA) only takes reports by phone between 9 am and 5 pm. The mayor of Santarém, João Leite, asked the Government to invest in environmental inspection and monitoring.

In March 2026, CLAPA, now CLAPA, Associação Popular Ecológica, became active again with a new board, to monitor pollution, raise public awareness and work with public and scientific bodies to protect the Alviela.
