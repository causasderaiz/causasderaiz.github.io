Hyperion II: second data center advances next to the Pego power plant without a complete environmental impact study

Abrantes, next to the Pego power plant, Santarém district

⚡ Quercus: combined consumption could reach nearly 19 times the municipality's annual electricity use

Second large-scale data center next to the Pego power plant, promoted by the Hyperion group, with over 151,000 m² of footprint: the council unanimously approved the preliminary information request before a complete environmental impact study existed. Quercus warns that, added to the already-approved EDC ONE data center in the same municipality, combined consumption could reach about 841 GWh/year in a conservative 30%-utilization scenario (5.7 times Abrantes' 2024 annual electricity use), or about 2.8 TWh/year at the maximum announced capacity (nearly 19 times), in a Tagus basin already facing a projected 40% water scarcity index for 2028-2033; the association demands a cumulative EIA that also assesses noise pollution and the heat-island effect.

Hyperion II Renewables Services wants to build the second large-scale data center next to the Pego power plant, in Abrantes, already holding National Interest Project (PIN) status. The project envisages a 200 MVA connection to the future Abrantes switching station, on over 1 million square metres of land (151,000 m² footprint, 287,000 m² built area), with a planned start in 2029.

Abrantes Municipal Council unanimously approved the project's preliminary request in May 2026. The Portuguese Environment Agency made its approval conditional on the submission of a complete environmental impact study, but only at the next licensing stage: in other words, the process moved forward before a full environmental assessment of the project existed.

On 26 August 2026, Quercus warned that this project, combined with the already-approved EDC ONE (300 MW, also at Pego), could raise combined electricity consumption to about 2.8 TWh/year at maximum announced capacity, nearly 19 times the municipality's current consumption (148.8 GWh in 2024), or 841 GWh/year (5.7 times) under a more conservative 30%-average-utilization scenario. Rigor note: press coverage of this Quercus calculation (including Ambiente Magazine) attributes the Alvega project a maximum capacity of just 20 MW, a figure that contradicts the 200 MVA connection cited by the council itself and by Correio do Ribatejo; it was not possible to confirm which figure is correct, so the multipliers above should be read as Quercus's published calculation, not as an independently verified fact.

PIN status speeds up licensing and gives the developer a single point of contact within the State. It is the same regime used for other data centers, mines and energy projects in Portugal.
