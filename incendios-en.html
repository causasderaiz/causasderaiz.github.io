2026 wildfire wave: Vouzela becomes Europe's largest fire of the year

North and Centre: Vouzela, Torre de Moncorvo, Valpaços, Mirandela, Águeda

⚡ Over 65,000 hectares burned by end of August

Over 65,000 hectares burned by the end of August, 58% above the ten-year average. The Vouzela fire, which started on 2 July, is officially Europe's largest fire of 2026, accounting for around 70% of all area burned in the country.

By 19 August 2026, Portugal had recorded 6,666 rural fires and 63,347 hectares of burned area, according to the Integrated Rural Fire Management System (SGIFR), run by AGIF. It is the fifth worst year for burned area in the decade, behind 2017, 2025, 2016 and 2022, and the sixth worst year for number of fires, reversing the downward trend seen since 2022.

The fire that broke out in Tourelhe, Vouzela, on 2 July officially became Europe's largest wildfire of 2026, alone accounting for around 70% of all area burned in Portugal this year, with the destruction nearly quadrupling compared to the equivalent 2025 fire. According to Diário de Notícias, this is already the worst cumulative record since the tragic year of 2017 and the Pedrógão Grande fire.

Since 15 August, several significant fires have broken out daily, mostly in the north, most notably in Torre de Moncorvo, Bragança district, the longest-running and still active. The municipalities with the most burned area in 2026 are Valpaços, Mirandela, Águeda and Vouzela. In July, a firefighter was injured while fighting the Santo Tirso fire and was taken to Hospital do Médio Ave.

Agricultural burning was the leading cause of fires recorded up to 15 August, responsible for 37% of incidents. By region, the North has the most burned area (34,929 ha), followed by the Centre (20,452 ha) and the Alentejo (3,335 ha).
