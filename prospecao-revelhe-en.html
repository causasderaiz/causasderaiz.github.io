Revelhe: mining prospecting request in Montesinho Natural Park

Santalha, Tuizelo and other parishes of Vinhais, Bragança district

◐ Public consultation closed with 157 submissions · ICNF and town councils issue unfavourable opinions

The same company, GMR - Consultores, is asking for prospecting and research rights over gold, silver, copper, lead and zinc deposits across a 37.8 km2 area in five parishes of Vinhais, inside Montesinho Natural Park and the UNESCO-recognised Transboundary Biosphere Reserve of Meseta Ibérica. The ICNF and the Bragança and Vinhais town councils issued unfavourable opinions; the public consultation gathered 157 submissions.

The "Revelhe" request (MNPPP613), filed by the same company, GMR - Consultores, Lda., asks for prospecting and research rights over gold, silver, copper, lead, zinc and associated mineral deposits across a 37.8 km2 area in five parishes of Vinhais: Santalha, Tuizelo, Candedo, Vilar de Ossos and the Sobreiró de Baixo e Alvaredos union of parishes. The area lies inside Montesinho Natural Park and the UNESCO-recognised Transboundary Biosphere Reserve of Meseta Ibérica.

The ICNF and the Bragança and Vinhais town councils issued unfavourable opinions on the request. The public consultation, open from 16 September to 25 October 2024, gathered 157 submissions on the participa.pt portal and formally closed on 25 February 2025, with no final decision made public so far by the Directorate-General for Energy and Geology.

Sara Riso, spokesperson for the UIVO movement, says the ICNF's unfavourable opinion was only filed on the last day of the Revelhe consultation, when it should have been available from the start, and questions the absence of an environmental mitigation plan or a benefit-sharing strategy with local communities. Streams that supply water to communities in the region rise within the project area, in a district already under tight irrigation management because of drought.

For mobilised residents, the two requests, Revelhe and Valongo 2, cover roughly 140 km2 combined and represent the opening of a "Pandora's box" toward a future mining phase, incompatible with the nature-tourism and young-family-retention strategy pursued by the Terras de Trás-os-Montes Intermunicipal Community.
