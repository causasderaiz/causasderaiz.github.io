Citizens contest Lisbon City Council's neglect of the historic Tapada das Necessidades garden

Tapada das Necessidades, Estrela, Lisbon

⚡ Rehabilitation handed to Lisboa Ocidental SRU, not yet started

Tapada das Necessidades, about 10 hectares of walled 18th-century garden in Estrela, listed as a Property of Public Interest, was granted by Lisbon City Council in 2016 to Banana Café Emporium for a restaurant, kiosk, offices and coworking. On 8 March 2021 the Friends of Tapada das Necessidades launched a petition against the planned demolitions, delivered to the Assembly of the Republic and now with over 12,000 signatures. The €19.26 million from the tourist tax approved in 2025 was taken away from the Lisbon Tourism Association in 2026; the works passed to Lisboa Ocidental SRU, the municipal urban regeneration company, and have not started.

What is at stake

Tapada das Necessidades was begun in 1742 next to the Necessidades Palace and is also known as the Royal Quinta of Necessidades. It covers about 10 walled hectares, with the orange grove, the box parterre, the greenhouse, the ponds and the former zoo. In 2016 Lisbon City Council granted the buildings in concession to Banana Café Emporium. The project included a restaurant in the former zoo, a kiosk with a terrace on the central lawn, and offices, coworking, an auditorium and an events space to the north. In 2024 the council proposed revoking that concession and approving the Safeguarding and Management Plan. Since then the rehabilitation has changed hands twice and has not started.

The garden and its trees. The garden is listed as a Property of Public Interest and has a Special Protection Zone. It has wild olive trees on the upper ground, pines and cypresses lining the wall, the box parterre, the orange grove and the cactus garden. The council itself says the site is badly degraded and needs a thorough rehabilitation.

The people who use the Tapada. The toilet by the south gate has been closed since a storm in winter 2025. According to the Friends of the Tapada, the taps have no water, there are dead plants and the paving and statues are degraded. It is one of the few large gardens in Alcântara and Estrela, open to the people who walk there every day.

Public heritage. According to the 2021 petition, the concession specifications planned to demolish the central part of the former zoo and several buildings to the north, to build a restaurant with a basement, an interpretation centre and an amphitheatre. The council said at the time that only the former Forest Services House would be demolished.
