Foupana Dam: new reservoir threatens the Algarve's last free-flowing stream

Castro Marim and Alcoutim, eastern Algarve

🗳 Public consultation until 12 October

Portugal's Environment Agency (APA) opened public consultation on the Environmental Impact Assessment for a new dam on the Foupana stream, promoted by the Eastern Algarve Irrigation Association, estimated to cost between 150 and 200 million euros. The NGO Almargem opposes the project, calling the Foupana "the Algarve's last great free-flowing stream" and habitat for the saramugo, a critically endangered fish; it notes the region's six existing dams already run below capacity and proposes alternatives such as wastewater reuse.

Portugal's Environment Agency (APA) opened, on 31 August 2026, public consultation on the Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) for the new Foupana dam in eastern Algarve, running until 12 October via the Participa portal. The project, promoted by the Eastern Algarve Irrigation Beneficiaries Association (ABPRSA), includes the dam itself, a connecting tunnel to the Odeleite-Beliche system and access roads, with an overall estimated cost between 150 and 200 million euros.

Environmental group Almargem has opposed the project since 2023, calling the Foupana stream "the Algarve's last great free-flowing stream" and one of the region's best-preserved watercourses, with no protection status at all. The group argues the Foupana should be included in the Natura 2000 network, warns of the impact on the saramugo, a critically endangered endemic fish, and notes that the Algarve's six existing dams (Odelouca, Arade, Funcho, Bravura, Beliche and Odeleite) already frequently run below capacity, questioning the cost-benefit of a seventh. Castro Marim's municipal council has separately warned about the lack of protected status for the area, compounded by a mining prospecting request overlapping the future dam's site.
