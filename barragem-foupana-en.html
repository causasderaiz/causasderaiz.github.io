No. 38 ⚡ Active situation Dam

Eastern Algarve irrigators plan the Foupana dam, in Castro Marim and Alcoutim, and the public consultation draws 421 submissions

Castro Marim and Alcoutim, eastern Algarve

⚡ Public consultation closed on 16 October with 421 submissions · Quercus, ZERO and Plataforma Água Sustentável call for an unfavourable decision

The Foupana dam, promoted by the eastern Algarve irrigators' association at an estimated cost of 150 to 200 million euros, threatens "the Algarve's last great free-flowing stream", according to Almargem, and the saramugo, a critically endangered fish. The public consultation closed on 16 October 2026 with 421 submissions; Quercus, ZERO and Plataforma Água Sustentável asked APA for an unfavourable decision and propose alternatives such as cutting network losses and reusing treated wastewater.

What is at stake

The Foupana stream, between Alcoutim and Castro Marim, is one of the best preserved watercourses in the Algarve and habitat of the saramugo, a critically endangered endemic fish, with no protected status. The Eastern Algarve Irrigation Beneficiaries Association wants to build a dam there, with a tunnel linking it to the Odeleite-Beliche system and access roads, at an estimated total cost of 150 to 200 million euros.

The jarabugo. It lives only in the Guadiana basin and is Endangered in the Red Book of Freshwater and Diadromous Fishes of mainland Portugal (2023). ZERO notes that it left the Critically Endangered category because the method changed, not because the risk fell, and that the developer's own study admits it will not survive the dam.

The other fish. Almargem and the Plataforma Água Sustentável also point to the Iberian long-snout barbel, the southern straight-mouth nase, the Iberian arched-mouth nase, the allis shad and the lamprey, at risk of local extinction.

The stream and its banks. It is one of the last large undammed streams in the Algarve, with sage-leaved willow thickets, oleander, tamujo and tamarisk, holm oak and cork oak woods and stands of Mediterranean juniper, a threatened formation. ZERO counts more than 70 hectares of holm and cork oaks affected.

The lynx and the Guadiana. The Plataforma Água Sustentável warns of a direct threat to the Guadiana Special Area of Conservation and the loss of the investment in Iberian lynx conservation. ZERO points to effects on the international stretch of the Guadiana, its estuary and the coast.

  1. 16 Oct

    On the last day of the consultation, Quercus and ZERO issue unfavourable opinions. Quercus sees the last great free ecological corridor of the eastern Algarve threatened; both propose cutting network losses, reusing wastewater and climate-adapted crops.

    Source: Ambiente Magazine ↗
  2. 16 Oct

    ZERO stresses that the Environmental Impact Study itself admits the saramugo will not survive the project and asks for clarification on possible conflicts of interest between AdP AQUA, in charge of the works, and the association promoting it.

    Source: Barlavento ↗
  3. 15 Oct

    Plataforma Água Sustentável asks APA to issue an unfavourable Environmental Impact Statement, calling the dam an "outdated and expensive model" and warning of a "direct threat to the Guadiana Special Area of Conservation".

    Source: Jornal do Algarve ↗
  4. 14 Oct

    The Platform for Sustainability and Biodiversity of the Algarve and Alentejo opposes the dam, calls for adapting the farming model in a region facing desertification and points to a "clear conflict of interest" of Macário Correia, linked to the irrigators and head of AdP Aqua.

    Source: Portugal Resident ↗
  5. 31 Aug to 16 Oct

    APA opens the public consultation on the dam's Environmental Impact Assessment on the Participa portal, from 31 August to 16 October 2026. It closes with 421 submissions.

    Source: Participa ↗
  6. 29 Aug

    The estimated total cost of the works is reported at between 150 and 200 million euros.

    Source: Algarve Marafado (estimated project cost) ↗
