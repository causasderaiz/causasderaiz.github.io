Foupana Dam: new reservoir threatens the Algarve's last free-flowing stream

Castro Marim and Alcoutim, eastern Algarve

◐ Public consultation closed on 16 October with 421 submissions · Quercus, ZERO and Plataforma Água Sustentável call for an unfavourable decision

The Foupana dam, promoted by the eastern Algarve irrigators' association at an estimated cost of 150 to 200 million euros, threatens "the Algarve's last great free-flowing stream", according to Almargem, and the saramugo, a critically endangered fish. The public consultation closed on 16 October 2026 with 421 submissions; Quercus, ZERO and Plataforma Água Sustentável asked APA for an unfavourable decision and propose alternatives such as cutting network losses and reusing treated wastewater.

What is at stake

The Foupana stream, between Alcoutim and Castro Marim, is one of the best preserved watercourses in the Algarve and habitat of the saramugo, a critically endangered endemic fish, with no protected status. The Eastern Algarve Irrigation Beneficiaries Association wants to build a dam there, with a tunnel linking it to the Odeleite-Beliche system and access roads, at an estimated total cost of 150 to 200 million euros.

  3. 15 Oct

    Plataforma Água Sustentável asks APA to issue an unfavourable Environmental Impact Statement, calling the dam an "outdated and expensive model" and warning of a "direct threat to the Guadiana Special Area of Conservation".

    Source: Jornal do Algarve ↗
  4. 14 Oct

    The Platform for Sustainability and Biodiversity of the Algarve and Alentejo opposes the dam, calls for adapting the farming model in a region facing desertification and points to a "clear conflict of interest" of Macário Correia, linked to the irrigators and head of AdP Aqua.

    Source: Portugal Resident ↗
  5. 31 Aug to 16 Oct

    APA opens the public consultation on the dam's Environmental Impact Assessment on the Participa portal, from 31 August to 16 October 2026. It closes with 421 submissions.

    Source: Participa ↗

    The estimated total cost of the works is reported at between 150 and 200 million euros.
