Sotavento Landfill: Cortelha (Loulé) demands the new cell be the last

Cortelha, Salir parish, Loulé municipality, Algarve

⚡ Conditional approval for the new Cell D; Loulé Municipal Assembly demands the landfill close after it

The Sotavento Landfill, in Cortelha (Loulé), takes the rubbish of eight municipalities in the eastern Algarve and is close to full. Residents complain of foul smells, lorries and risks to water; in June 2026, Loulé Municipal Assembly unanimously demanded that the new Cell D be the last. The CCDR Algarve gave Cell D conditional approval, and ZERO warns that landfilling untreated organic waste is illegal and calls for more mechanical-biological treatment.

The Sotavento Landfill, run by Algar, is in Cortelha, in the interior of Loulé municipality, and takes waste from Alcoutim, Castro Marim, Faro, Loulé, Olhão, São Brás de Alportel, Tavira and Vila Real de Santo António. According to barlavento, it receives about 150,000 tonnes a year and the cell in use was close to full.

In May 2022, ZERO accused the landfill of operating "illegally since 2017", because its expansion with cell C, built in 2017 and 2018, had not undergone an Environmental Impact Assessment. The CCDR Algarve replied that the APA environmental licence was valid until 16 August 2026 and that ICNF had found it unnecessary to subject cell C to an assessment.

From 23 December 2025 to 4 February 2026, the environmental impact study for a new Cell D, promoted by Algar, was under public consultation. According to ZERO, 152 of the submissions (97.44%) opposed the project.

On 22 June 2026, Loulé Municipal Assembly unanimously approved a motion from the PS demanding that the landfill close for good after Cell D, a written commitment to that effect, a study to move waste management out of the municipality, an environmental rehabilitation plan for Cortelha, and independent monitoring of groundwater and soils. According to Algarve Marafado, residents complain of a "nauseating smell, growing lorry traffic and a constant threat to water resources, flora and fauna".

The CCDR Algarve's Environmental Impact Declaration for Cell D, made public in October 2026, is conditionally favourable and provides for capacity for about 875,000 tonnes, 150,000 tonnes a year and an estimated life of five to eight years. The declaration notes that landfilling waste with an unstabilised organic fraction is illegal under the legal regime for landfill. ZERO, which opposes the incinerator planned for the Algarve, argues that "the new Cell D will have to be the end of the line for waste, not the destination for mixed waste", and proposes expanding mechanical-biological treatment at Algar's plants.
